# The Long Season Begins: PFL Week 1, 2026

*Narrated, as all things worth hearing are, slowly.*

Every year, at the end of the warm months, twelve men gather at the edge of the
continent and begin a march they cannot see the end of. They have prepared.
They have studied. They have, in some cases, drafted two kickers. And none of
it — not one page of it — matters until the first Sunday arrives and the ledger
opens.

The ledger opened. Let me tell you what it said.

## Dan 168, Joe 137.5 — The Loudest Room in the League

There is a peculiar cruelty in fantasy football, and Joe found it on the very
first day of the very first week. He scored 137.5 points. That was the second
most in the entire league. In eleven other lives, in eleven other matchups, that
number is a win and a handshake and a good night's sleep.

Instead he ran into Dan, and Dan scored 168.

Caleb Williams put up **38 points** — tied for the highest score of the week,
and the beating heart of the highest-scoring game on the board. Behind him came
Kenneth Walker III with **28** and David Montgomery with **27**, three men from
one roster inside the week's top ten. The scouts had looked at Dan's team in the
summer and handed him a **C+**, muttering about Diggs and Deebo and Rodgers and
the oldest core in the league. And sure enough — Stefon Diggs **11**, Aaron
Rodgers **11**, Deebo Samuel Sr. **8**. The old men did their quiet work. The
young ones did the rest.

Joe, graded **A-**, got exactly what an A- is supposed to give you: Patrick
Mahomes **24**, Jonathan Taylor **22**, Jordan Love **22**, Isaiah Likely
**22**, Ladd McConkey **14**. Four men at 22 or better. A beautifully built
house, burned down by a man with a **C+** next to his name.

I've seen a lot of things in this league. I have never seen a grade cash a
check.

## Aaron 134, Sean 109 — Two Cannons, One Winner

Josh Allen scored **38**. Same as Caleb Williams. Same as the top of the
mountain. And for a while it looked like Sean had an answer, because Derrick
Henry went for **33** and Jahmyr Gibbs went for **28** and Chuba Hubbard added
**16** — the six-back bet, paying out exactly as drawn up.

But Sean built a wall of running backs in a league that pays quarterbacks, and
when the bill came due his quarterback room offered Kirk Cousins and **15
points**. Held together with tape and prayer, they said in the spring. The tape
held. The prayer did not.

Aaron, meanwhile, got **25** from Jalen Coker — a name most of this league could
not have picked out of a lineup in August — plus Amon-Ra St. Brown **19**, Chris
Olave **12**, Chase Brown **11**, Juwan Johnson **11**. And Cam Little kicked in
**6**, which is the sort of thing that happens when a man drafts two kickers and
refuses to apologize for it. Aaron 134, Sean 109.

## Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5 — The Only Game That Breathed

Five of this week's six games were settled with room to spare. One was not.

Bruce spent draft weekend watching nine quarterbacks come off the board in the
first round and passing on every one of them, and the league called it guilt. On
Sunday, Tyler Shough scored **30 points**. Deshaun Watson added **11**. Bijan
Robinson ran for **20**. It was not beautiful — 91 points is 91 points, and both
these teams finished well shy of the league average of **111.58** — but it was
enough by **7.5**, the only single-digit margin of the week.

Mitch had the better quarterbacks and the worse Sunday: Trevor Lawrence **26**,
Lamar Jackson **22**, Breece Hall **17**, Kyren Williams **8**, Tyler Bass
**7.5**, Tee Higgins **3**. Six men scored. Everyone else stood in the cold and
watched. Eight more points anywhere on that list and Mitch is 1-0 and the whole
mood of his autumn changes.

He is 0-1 instead. That's how it goes. The margin between hope and the other
thing is usually about the size of a field goal.

## Matt 130.5, Mike 120 — The Man With No Tight Ends

They said Matt drafted nineteen players and not a single tight end, and that
someone, eventually, would charge him rent for one. Week 1 sent no invoice.
Bryce Young — *Bryce Young* — scored **35 points**. Christian Watson added
**25**. Jalen Hurts **21**, Nico Collins **14**, Denzel Boston **11**. Saquon
Barkley managed **6** and Matt won anyway, 130.5 to 120.

Mike had his own fireworks. D'Andre Swift went for **33**, fourth-best in the
league this week. Justin Jefferson **24**. Joe Burrow **14**, Baker Mayfield
**14**, Parker Washington **14**. He needed 10.5 more points from somewhere —
from Matthew Golden's **6**, from DeVonta Smith's **3**, from Cameron Dicker's
lonely **1** — and the somewhere never came.

## Chris 111.5, Eli 91.5 — The Broken Shape Holds

They gave Chris a **D+**. Five tight ends, two receivers, a roster built like a
house with five kitchens and no front door. And then Sunday arrived and the
kitchens started cooking: Dallas Goedert **22**, Trey McBride **14**, Tyler
Warren **8**. Jared Goff **16**, Cam Skattebo **14**, Malik Willis **14**, Evan
McPherson **9.5**, the San Francisco defense **5**. Christian McCaffrey, the
crown jewel, contributed a whisper — **3 points** — and it didn't matter at all.

Eli, who bet his future on 2028, got a glimpse of it early: Jaxson Dart **24**,
Kyle Monangai **17**, Dak Prescott **16**, the Pittsburgh defense **8**. But
Puka Nacua managed **3** and Alec Pierce managed **3**, and 91.5 doesn't win
many Sundays in this league. Chris by 20.

## Pete 104.5, Cal 58 — The Week's Cold Water

And now the hard part.

Cal walked out of the draft with the only **A** in the room. Stars early,
starters late, the grown-up build, the team you'd bet the house on. On the first
Sunday of the season Cal scored **58 points** — the lowest total in the league
by a comfortable distance — and lost by **46.5**, the widest margin of the week.

Ashton Jeanty did his part with **25**. Jayden Daniels put up **10**, CeeDee
Lamb **8**, and after that the roster turned into a row of identical threes:
Bhayshul Tuten **3**, Rome Odunze **3**, Tyler Allgeier **3**. A great team,
briefly, played like a small one.

Pete — graded **C+**, mocked all summer for owning the best receiver room and no
backfield — simply let his receivers be receivers. Zay Flowers **20**, Jaxon
Smith-Njigba **17**, Rashee Rice **8**, with Brock Purdy **21** and C.J. Stroud
**19** under center and Jacory Croskey-Merritt turning up from nowhere for
**11**. Pete 104.5, and a statement made in the first week of a long year.

## Where It Leaves Us

The standings are sorted by cumulative points, and after one week they read like
a photograph nobody has had time to argue with. Dan sits alone at the top with
**168**. Joe is second at **137.5** — second in the league in scoring, 0-1 in the
standings, a sentence that should be framed and hung somewhere. Aaron **134**,
Matt **130.5**, Mike **120**, Chris **111.5**, Sean **109**, Pete **104.5**, Eli
**91.5**, Bruce **91**, Mitch **83.5**, and Cal, at the bottom, with **58**.

No one has moved yet. There is nothing to move from. Six men are 1-0 with a
one-game winning streak; six are 0-1 with a losing one. Every matchup this week
was a first meeting — no history, no grudges, no debts. Just first impressions,
and first impressions, as anyone who has ever done time in a twelve-team league
will tell you, are worth exactly what you paid for them.

*I hope.*
