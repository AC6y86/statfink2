# This Was Their First Hour — An Address to the House on Week 1, 2026

*Delivered to the Members of the Peninsula Football League, assembled.*

Gentlemen of the League — I have nothing to offer you but blood, toil, tears, and a waiver wire. We have come through the long, dry summer of speculation, through the mock drafts and the grand designs, and we arrive at last upon the only ground that matters: the scoreboard. It is a hard ground. It does not care what grade your draft received in the spring.

Let us be candid at the outset. The League averaged **111.58 points** this week. Six armies crossed that line. Six did not. And in the space between those two facts lies the whole story of the first week of the war.

---

## I. The Northern Front: Dan 168, Joe 137.5

Never in the field of fantasy conflict has so much been scored by so many, to the ruin of so few. Dan marched out **168 points** — the highest total in the League — and Joe answered with **137.5**, a figure that would have defeated every other owner in the competition, and defeated nobody at all.

Dan's assault was led by **Caleb Williams, 38 points**, with **Kenneth Walker III at 28** and **David Montgomery at 27** coming up behind him in column. Three men in the week's top ten, drawn from a single order of battle. There were reinforcements besides — **DJ Moore 17**, **Javonte Williams 16**, and the old guard of **Stefon Diggs 11** and **Aaron Rodgers 11**, veterans of a former campaign, still in the line, still firing.

And here I must say a word about the critics. Dan was graded **C+** in the spring; he was told he had bet on 2022. He has replied with 168. Joe, graded **A-**, brought **Patrick Mahomes 24**, **Jonathan Taylor 22**, **Jordan Love 22**, and **Isaiah Likely 22** — four men at twenty-two or better — and goes into the record as **0-1**. It is the oldest lesson of warfare: you may fight magnificently and still lose the day, because the enemy was fighting magnificently somewhat harder. The margin was **30.5**.

## II. The Southern Coast: Pete 104.5, Cal 58

Some blowouts are unfortunate. This one was a demolition conducted at leisure.

Pete, that **C+** man with, we were told, the best receiver room and no backfield, won by **46.5 points** — the widest gap in the League. **Brock Purdy 21**, **Zay Flowers 20**, **C.J. Stroud 19**, and **Jaxon Smith-Njigba 17** formed a broad, unhurried advance; even the supposedly absent backfield produced **Jacory Croskey-Merritt, 11**.

Against this came Cal — the **A** grade, the "adult build," the stars-early construction admired by every armchair strategist in the realm. Cal mustered **58**. **Ashton Jeanty ran for 25** and looked about him for an army that never arrived: **Jayden Daniels 10**, **CeeDee Lamb 8**, and then a long, melancholy procession of threes. Dan's Caleb Williams, by himself, scored **38**. Cal's entire assembly scored **58**. Let that comparison stand as the monument to the week.

To Cal I say only this: success is not final, failure is not fatal, and it is September. Keep buggering on.

## III. The Shootout at the Crossroads: Aaron 134, Sean 109

Two cannonades, one victor. **Josh Allen put up 38** for Aaron — equal to the highest individual mark in the League — and **Jalen Coker's 25** was the sort of unheralded flanking manoeuvre that wins wars and ruins reputations. **Amon-Ra St. Brown added 19**, **Chris Olave 12**, and Aaron took it **134 to 109**, a margin of **25**.

Sean did not go quietly. **Derrick Henry 33** and **Jahmyr Gibbs 28** — the very backfield thesis he drafted, vindicated in its particulars — with **Chuba Hubbard 16** behind them. But Sean's quarterback room, described in the spring as held together with tape and prayer, offered **Kirk Cousins, 15**, and the prayer was not answered. The **A-** grade falls to **0-1**. Aaron, meanwhile, drafted two kickers and needed only one: **Cam Little, 6**.

## IV. The Battle of the Narrow Margin: Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5

Here was the week's only close-run thing — **7.5 points**, the sole matchup decided inside a single score.

Observe the arithmetic and marvel. Bruce won with **91 points**, the tenth-best weekly total in a twelve-owner league. Eli scored **91.5** and lost. Such is fortune; such is the schedule; such is the cruel mathematics of being paired against the right opponent at the right hour.

Bruce's salvation arrived from the least expected quarter. The man accused of **QB guilt** — who passed on nine first-round quarterbacks and then took Nix, Shough, and Watson — was rescued by **Tyler Shough, 30 points**, sixth-best in the League. **Bijan Robinson added 20**; **Deshaun Watson 11**. Mitch, for his part, ran two quarterbacks up the hill — **Trevor Lawrence 26** and **Lamar Jackson 22** — with **Breece Hall at 17**, and fell short by the width of a half-point twice repeated. Had Mitch found eight more points anywhere on that roster, we should be telling a different story. He did not. We are not.

## V. The Contest of Shapes: Matt 130.5, Mike 120

Two fine armies; one narrow door. Matt — the man with **zero tight ends**, an oddity that the League intends to charge him for — took it **130.5 to 120**, by **10.5**.

His instrument was **Bryce Young, 35 points**, third-highest in the League, with **Christian Watson 25** and **Jalen Hurts 21** in support and **Nico Collins 14** for good measure. Mike answered with **D'Andre Swift, 33** — fourth in the League — and **Justin Jefferson 24**, then twin quarterbacks at **14 apiece in Joe Burrow and Baker Mayfield**, and **Parker Washington 14**. He cleared the League average of 111.58 by a comfortable stretch and was beaten anyway. **Cameron Dicker's 1 point** from the kicking tee is the sort of detail one revisits at three in the morning for the remainder of a season.

## VI. The Cathedral of Tight Ends: Chris 111.5, Eli 91.5

They graded Chris **D+**. They said five tight ends and two receivers was a broken shape. Chris then went out and won **111.5 to 91.5** — a margin of **20** — on the backs of, I regret to inform the critics, his tight ends. **Dallas Goedert 22**, **Trey McBride 14**, **Tyler Warren 8**, with **Jared Goff 16**, **Cam Skattebo 14**, and **Malik Willis 14** filling the ranks, and **Evan McPherson 9.5** from the tee. The one disappointment in his column was **Christian McCaffrey, 3** — an elite piece that arrived at the front and did very little.

Eli, the man betting on 2028, got a glimpse of it: **Jaxson Dart 24**, **Kyle Monangai 17**, **Dak Prescott 16**, and a stout **Pittsburgh Steelers Defense, 8**. It was not yet enough. The future, by definition, has not arrived.

---

## The Ledger of the Realm

Dan sits **first**, **1-0**, with **168** cumulative — the only man above 140. Joe is **second** on **137.5** despite going **0-1**; Aaron **third** at **134**, Matt **fourth** at **130.5**, Mike **fifth** at **120** at **0-1**. Chris **sixth (111.5)**, Sean **seventh (109)**, Pete **eighth (104.5)** — a **1-0** man sitting eighth, which tells you everything about the difference between winning and scoring. Eli **ninth (91.5)**, Bruce **tenth (91)**, Mitch **eleventh (83.5)**, and Cal **twelfth** on **58**, the only owner in the League who failed to reach 80.

Six men carry a **W1** streak — Dan, Aaron, Matt, Chris, Pete, Bruce. Six carry an **L1** — Joe, Mike, Sean, Eli, Mitch, Cal. Every matchup was a first meeting; there are no grudges yet, only grievances in the making.

This is not the end. It is not even the beginning of the end. But it is, perhaps, the end of the beginning.
