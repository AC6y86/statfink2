# A House Divided by Half a Point: An Address on the Third Week of the Peninsula Football League

*Delivered from the telegraph office, where the President has sat up late reading the wires, 2026*

---

## I. The Address

Three weeks ago our owners brought forth upon this Peninsula a new season, conceived in trash talk and dedicated to the proposition that all rosters are created equal.

Now we are engaged in a great fantasy war, testing whether that season, or any season so conceived and so drafted, can long endure. We are met on a great battlefield of that war. It is altogether fitting and proper that we should take account of it, because the world will little note nor long remember what we say here, but it will never forget what Jahmyr Gibbs did.

The league averaged 103.92 points this week. Some men went well above that line and some fell well below it. Three are still perfect at 3-0: Aaron, Dan, and Pete. Two, Mitch and Eli, are 0-3. And one game, Mike against Chris, was settled by half a point, the smallest margin of this young season. A house divided against itself cannot stand. This one stood on a single half-point.

---

## II. Dispatches from the Telegraph Office

I have always found that the wires tell a man more truth than his generals do. Here is what came over them.

### Dispatch the First: Sean 141, Eli 72.5. The Rout at Gibbs's Crossing

Of all the wires this week, this one wanted reading twice. **Sean put up 141 points, the most in the league, and beat Eli by 68.5.** It was the widest margin of the week.

When critics came to me about General Grant, I told them, "I can't spare this man; he fights." I say the same of **Jahmyr Gibbs, whose 33 points were the best individual score in the league this week.** He was the whole cavalry. Behind him came a supply line that never ran short: Matthew Stafford 22, Derrick Henry 22, Kirk Cousins 21, Brock Bowers 17, DK Metcalf 8, Chuba Hubbard 6, Josh Downs 6, and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers Defense 5. Sean scored +24.33 above his own average, got his first win to go 1-2, and **climbed three places to third in cumulative points (350).** The draft ledger gave him an A-. Two weeks ago that grade looked like a joke. It looks sound now.

Eli had few defenses left. George Kittle held the line with 22 and Dak Prescott added 16, but after them came Jakobi Meyers 11, Will Reichard 7, George Pickens 6, and a kicker, Nick Folk, at 5.5 in the lineup. **Jaylen Waddle made the week's bust list with 2 points,** and Omarion Hampton added 3. Eli's 72.5 sits -11.5 below his own average. He is 0-3 and **slid two places to last in points at 252.** The draft book gave him a B+ and said he was building for 2028. Two years is a long time to wait.

### Dispatch the Second: Mike 104, Chris 103.5. The Half-Point Stand

Of all the telegrams this week, this is the one I read standing up.

**Mike won 104 to 103.5, a margin of 0.5 and the closest game of the season.** Mike won it with his quarterbacks. Jacoby Brissett gave him 27, the seventh-best performance in the league, and Geno Smith gave him 26. Behind them were Matthew Golden 17, kicker Harrison Mevis 9, Parker Washington 8, Rachaad White 8, D'Andre Swift 6, and DeVonta Smith 3. That was enough for Mike's first victory. He moved to 1-2 and **rose one place to eighth (297.67).**

Chris will be thinking about this one for a long time. Jared Goff scored 19, and Harold Fannin Jr., one of the five tight ends the draft ledger says he owns, matched him with 19. Christian McCaffrey and Michael Wilson each gave 14, Malik Willis 9, and the Denver Broncos Defense a respectable 8. The low end is where it went wrong: Trey McBride 6, Evan McPherson 5.5, and three men at 3 apiece in Travis Etienne Jr., Cam Skattebo, and Tyler Warren. **A single extra point from any one of them and the wire would have read the other way.** Chris scored +2.5 above his own average and lost anyway. He is 1-2 and stays seventh (303).

They say you can fool all of the people some of the time. Chris's lineup fooled no one this week. It came up half a point short.

### Dispatch the Third: Aaron 107, Matt 65. General McClellan's Army Would Not March

**Aaron beat Matt 107 to 65, a 42-point margin,** and did it in an off week. His 107 was -22 below his own average, and it still ranked fourth in the league. Josh Allen scored 22, Juwan Johnson 19, Luther Burden III 11, Cam Ward 11, Chris Olave 9, and Jaylen Warren 9. **Aaron is 3-0 with a streak of three wins, and he holds first in cumulative points at 387.** He held his position through a slow week, and that is worth something.

Of Matt I can only repeat what I once wrote to a slow general: *if you do not want to use the army, I should like to borrow it.* **Matt's 65 was the lowest score in the league, -32.39 below his own average,** and he **fell five places to ninth (292.17).** Christian Watson and Bryce Young each scored 14, Justin Herbert and Keaton Mitchell 11 each, Saquon Barkley only 6, and **Denzel Boston made the bust list with 2.** The draft ledger has noted all season that Matt carries zero tight ends, and Chris, who carries five, lost his own game by half a point. These two men should talk. He is still 2-1 on the strength of earlier wins.

### Dispatch the Fourth: Bruce 138.5, Cal 93.5. Three Men at Twenty-Eight or Better

**Bruce scored 138.5, second-best in the league, and beat Cal by 45.** Three of his men had among the six best scores in the league: Tyler Shough 29, Bijan Robinson 28, Bo Nix 28. Garrett Wilson added 17, Jeremiyah Love 14, Davante Adams 9, and Chris Boswell 7.5. Bruce was +22.67 above his own average. He is 2-1 and **rose one place to fourth (347.5).** The draft gave him a C+. He is winning anyway.

Cal fought better than the score shows. He was +6.5 above his own average. Ja'Marr Chase and Jordan Addison scored 14 apiece, CeeDee Lamb, Bhayshul Tuten, and Mike Washington Jr. each gave 11, and the Washington Commanders Defense gave 8. But Kyler Murray gave him only 5 at quarterback, in a league where the other side's quarterbacks put up 29 and 28. Cal's draft grade was an A, and he is 1-2. **He climbed one place to tenth (261).**

### Dispatch the Fifth: Pete 122, Joe 99. The Quiet Unbeaten

**Pete beat Joe 122 to 99, a margin of 23.** He did it with two quarterbacks and a receiver. Sam Darnold scored 32, the second-best score in the league. Brock Purdy scored 29, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba 25. Drake London added 12, Cooper Kupp 8, and Zay Flowers 6. The draft book called Pete's roster "best WR room, no backfield," and that held true: Ty Johnson and Najee Harris scored 3 each. It did not matter. Pete was +16.28 above his own average and is **3-0, up two places to sixth (317.17).** His record is better than his point total. I have known a few men in Illinois who got by the same way.

Joe, a good man and a friend of this office, took the loss. James Cook III scored 20, Jordan Love 19, Patrick Mahomes 16, Brian Robinson Jr. 11, Mike Evans and Chris Rodriguez Jr. 8 apiece, and the Jacksonville Jaguars Defense 5. Jonathan Taylor gave only 3 and Ladd McConkey 3. Joe's 99 was -11.5 below his own average. He is 1-2 and **fell two places to fifth (331.5).**

### Dispatch the Sixth: Dan 106.5, Mitch 94.5. The Grind Continues

**Dan beat Mitch 106.5 to 94.5, a 12-point margin.** The draft book said Dan was betting on 2022, and the old guard came through: Aaron Rodgers scored 24, the tenth-best performance of the week, and Travis Kelce added 11. Kenneth Walker III gave 19, Michael Penix Jr. 16, and Javonte Williams and Deebo Samuel Sr. 14 each. DJ Moore scored only 3. Dan's 106.5 was -10.5 below his own average, but he is **3-0 and holds second (351).** He was a C+ on draft night.

Mitch is having the hardest luck in this league. His 94.5 was +9.67 above his own average and ranked ninth of twelve, and he still lost. Trevor Lawrence scored 21, Lamar Jackson 19, Tee Higgins and Terry McLaurin 14 apiece, Wan'Dale Robinson 11, Kyren Williams 9, and Chase McLaughlin 6.5. **He is 0-3 but rose one place to eleventh (254.5),** above Eli. Mitch keeps scoring and keeps losing. Some weeks the wires carry nothing but bad news, and I have learned to keep reading them.

## IV. Benediction

With malice toward none, with charity for all, with firmness in the lineup as God gives us to see the lineup, let us strive on to finish the work we are in. To Aaron, Dan, and Pete: stay humble, because 4-0 is not promised. To Mitch and Eli: the better angels of your nature, and of your waiver wire, will come in time. To Chris: it was half a point, and you will remember it all autumn. To Matt: find a tight end. Chris has five.
