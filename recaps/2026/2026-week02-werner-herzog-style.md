# The Overwhelming Indifference of Week Two

*A documentary in six chapters, narrated against the wind.*

---

## Prologue: The Landscape

I have been asked to describe what happened in the Peninsula Football League in the second week of the year 2026, and I must begin by confessing that nothing *happened* at all. Points accumulated. Men in distant stadiums fell down in the grass. Twelve owners watched screens refresh themselves, and the screens did not love them back.

The league scored an average of 96.54 points this week. This number is not a triumph. It is a mean, a flat grey horizon, and against it every man measured himself and mostly found that he had failed. Nine of the twelve scored below their own season average. Nine. The universe does not conspire against these men — that would require interest, and the universe has none.

And everywhere, in every lineup, the same obscenity: the defenses. Houston, zero. Detroit, zero. Baltimore, zero. Cleveland, zero. Jacksonville, Kansas City, Buffalo, Tampa Bay, Denver, San Francisco, Chicago, New Orleans, Los Angeles, Pittsburgh, Philadelphia, Washington, New York, Dallas — all of them, zero. Grown units of grown men stood in the mud like oxen in a fog, and they did not even have the dignity to be defeated. They were simply *ignored*.

---

## Chapter One: The Man Who Ate the Sun — Aaron 146, Bruce 118

There is one exception to the misery, and I am obliged to report it honestly: Aaron.

Josh Allen scored **40 points**. Forty. Last week he scored thirty-eight, and I said then that it could not continue, that nature abhors such abundance — and nature, indifferent, simply handed him two more. Around this furnace Aaron arranged Amon-Ra St. Brown at **25**, Cam Ward at **22**, Chris Olave at **14**, Dalton Kincaid at **14**, Terrance Ferguson at **11**. One hundred forty-six points. Six above his own average, which is to say: the man is not surging. This is his *resting state*. This is what he does while sleeping. He is 2-0 and sits atop the league with **280 cumulative points**, first in a race no one else has entered.

Bruce scored 118. Under any other sky, 118 is a life's work. Davante Adams **28**, Tyler Shough **22**, Deshaun Watson **16**, Garrett Wilson **11** — Bruce exceeded his own average by 13.5 points, the second-best score in the entire league, and he lost by 28. He climbed five places in the standings, all the way to fifth, and *still* he lost. I find this the most honest lesson of the week: you may do everything correctly and be crushed anyway, and the mountain will not even notice that you were on it.

---

## Chapter Two: The Executioner's Arithmetic — Matt 96.67, Mitch 76.5

Matt is 2-0 and he does not own a single tight end. Two weeks have passed and the hole in his roster remains unrepaired, an open wound he refuses to dress, and it does not matter, because Bryce Young gave him **24** and Jalen Hurts gave him **19** and the Carolina Panthers Defense — alone among the drowned — rose from the swamp with **9.67**. Denzel Boston **14**. Tetairoa McMillan **9**. Christian Watson **8**. Ninety-six point six seven: sixteen and ninety-two hundredths *below* his own average, and it was enough by 20.17 points.

Mitch scored 76.5. Kyren Williams **14**, Lamar Jackson **11**, Sam LaPorta **11**, Dalton Schultz **9**, Chase McLaughlin **8.5** — a roster of competent men performing competently, adding up to nothing. He is 0-2. He sits twelfth in cumulative points with 160. His two defeats have been the most one-sided of the four winless. There is a particular sadness in a man who is not humiliated but merely *outlasted*, week after week, like a small animal that simply stops.

---

## Chapter Three: Two Points and Eighty-Three Hundredths — Dan 76.5, Mike 73.67

Here is where the fog is thickest.

Dan scored 168 in week one. This week: **76.5**. A collapse of stunning completeness — 45.75 points below his own average, the eleventh-best score among twelve teams. The old core he built his house upon — Travis Kelce **17**, Stefon Diggs **16**, Aaron Rodgers **6** — is aging in front of us, in real time, and I could hear the timbers groan. Kenneth Walker III gave **12**, Daniel Jones **11**, Harrison Butker **9.5**, and the Seahawks defense — a flicker of life in the dark — **5**.

And Dan *won*. He won by 2.83 points, because Mike managed only 73.67, the worst score in the league. DeVonta Smith **17**, Joe Burrow **16**, Baker Mayfield **11**, and New England's defense **9.67**, and then a long procession of threes: Achane 3, Jefferson 3, Swift 3, Golden 3. Harrison Mevis, the kicker, **2**.

Consider: had Mevis found three more points anywhere in that stadium — a single additional kick through the uprights, a tiny act of geometry — Mike would have won. He did not. Mike falls four places to ninth, 0-2, the twelfth-best score of the week. Dan remains unbeaten while slipping from first to second. Two men, both diminished, and the one who fell further was rewarded. There is no harmony here. There is only accounting.

---

## Chapter Four: The Five Tight Ends — Joe 95, Chris 88

Chris owns five tight ends and two receivers. He is, by universal agreement, the man Matt should trade with, and neither of them moves, because men prefer their familiar suffering to the terror of changing it.

Jared Goff gave Chris **32**. Christian McCaffrey **16**, Malik Willis **11**, Trey McBride **8**, Tyler Warren **8**. And then, in the cold at the bottom of the column, the week's one true bust: **Travis Etienne Jr., 2 points**. Two. A running back who ran, and the running meant nothing. He is the lesson of the entire week compressed into a single line of a spreadsheet.

Joe won with 95 — twenty-one and a quarter below his own average — on Patrick Mahomes **27**, Jonathan Taylor **22**, James Cook III **17**, and then a thinning air: Doubs 6, Evans 3, Robinson 3. Seven points separated them. Joe is 1-1 and slips to third; Chris is 1-1 and slips to seventh. Both were worse than they are capable of being. One of them is happier. This is not justice; it is weather.

---

## Chapter Five: The Cruelest Margin — Pete 90.67, Eli 88

Two point six seven.

Pete's celebrated receiver room produced Jaxon Smith-Njigba at **36 points** — the second-highest score of the week — and Brock Purdy at **27**, and C.J. Stroud at **12**, and still Pete needed the Minnesota Vikings Defense to scrape **1.67** out of the dirt to survive. One and sixty-seven hundredths. The margin was 2.67. Without that trembling fraction, the week ends differently and a man's season turns over in the night. Pete is 2-0, graded C+ in the draft, and eighth in cumulative points with 195.17 — an unbeaten man whom the numbers themselves do not believe in. Last week he obliterated Cal by 46.5. This week he survived by a decimal. The gods do not vary their cruelty; they vary only its scale.

And Eli. Dak Prescott **29**, Omarion Hampton **14**, TreVeyon Henderson **14**, George Kittle **14**, Jaylen Waddle **9**. Eighty-eight points — only 1.75 below his own average, which is to say: Eli was *himself*, entirely and completely, and being himself was not enough by less than three points. Nick Folk, the second kicker, scored **2**. He is 0-2, tenth, on a two-game losing streak, and the injuries are arriving like crows on a wire. I will speak of them at the end.

---

## Chapter Six: The Man Who Rose and Was Still Eleventh — Cal 109.5, Sean 100

Cal scored 109.5, an astonishing 25.75 above his own average — CeeDee Lamb **28**, Ja'Marr Chase **22**, Jake Ferguson **16**, Bhayshul Tuten **11**, and three separate eights from Lloyd, Allen, and a quarterback named Jayden Daniels who contributed his **8** before the darkness took him. Cal is 1-1. The third-best score of the entire week, and he is ranked **eleventh** in cumulative points with 167.5. He was graded A in the draft. The jungle did not read the grades.

Sean was graded A-. Sean scored 100 points — Matthew Stafford **32**, Kirk Cousins **24**, Jahmyr Gibbs **14**, Derrick Henry **11**, Chuba Hubbard **11** — a quarterback room held together with tape and prayer, performing miracles, and Sean is **0-2**. He has scored 100 or more in both weeks of his life and has nothing to show for either. He lost by 9.5. He climbed to sixth in cumulative points, level with Bruce at 209, and his record remains empty. I have seen men freeze to death on mountains while walking the correct path, and on a spreadsheet it looks exactly like this.

Four men are unbeaten: Aaron, Dan, Matt, Pete. Four men are winless: Sean, Mike, Eli, Mitch. Four men hang suspended exactly in the middle at 1-1: Joe, Bruce, Chris, Cal. The league has arranged itself into three perfect groups of four, as if by design — but there is no design. It is coincidence wearing the mask of meaning, and next week it will take the mask off and show us nothing at all underneath.

Aaron sits at 280 cumulative points. Mitch sits at 160. Between them stretches a distance that no one will cross this month, and the wind blows across it, and the wind does not care, and the screens refresh, and the screens do not love them back.
