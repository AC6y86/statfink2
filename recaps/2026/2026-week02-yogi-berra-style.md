# Week 2: "It Ain't Over Till The Waiver Deadline Is Over"

*Twelve managers, six games, and half of 'em decided by a sneeze. I never said most of the things I'm about to say.*

---

## First Thing First, Which Is Usually Second

The league averaged **96.54 points** this week, which don't mean much, on account of almost nobody scored 96.54. That's the thing about averages — everybody's either above 'em or below 'em, and the ones in the middle are usually somewhere else.

Four of the six games landed inside ten points. When four games are that close, you can observe a lot by watching, and what you'd have observed is twelve grown men watching a kicker.

---

## Aaron 146, Bruce 118 — You Can't Win With Points If The Other Guy Has More

Bruce scored **118**. That's **+13.5 over his own average**, the second-best score in the whole league this week, and he lost by **28**. Bruce didn't do anything wrong except play Aaron. Davante Adams went for **28**, Tyler Shough — Tyler Shough! — put up **22**, Deshaun Watson added **16**, Garrett Wilson **11**, Aaron Jones Sr. **9**. Then three fellas in a row chipped in **8** apiece — Emeka Egbuka, Jonah Coleman, Tank Bigsby — like they'd agreed on it beforehand. Bruce had deep depth. Depth don't matter when the other fella's got altitude.

Because Aaron's got **Josh Allen**, and Josh Allen went for **40**. That's forty. That's after thirty-eight last week. Two weeks, two rocket ships, and I'm told the future ain't what it used to be, but Aaron's future looks exactly like his past, which is the problem for everybody else. Amon-Ra St. Brown chucked in **25**, Cam Ward **22**, and Chris Olave and Dalton Kincaid went **14** apiece like twins who never met. Terrance Ferguson **11**. **146** total, **+6 over his own average**, best score in the league, and Aaron climbed **▲2 to 1st with 280 cumulative points** — the only man in the building past 280.

Bijan Robinson had **3**. Even the good ones have Tuesdays on Sunday.

## Matt 96.67, Mitch 76.5 — He Ain't Got Tight Ends And He Don't Need None

Matt still doesn't own a tight end. Two weeks now. Everybody keeps telling him about it and he keeps winning anyway, which is the worst thing you can do to good advice. **Bryce Young 24**, **Jalen Hurts 19**, rookie Denzel Boston **14**, and the **Carolina Panthers Defense 9.67** — a defense outscoring most people's best receiver. Tetairoa McMillan **9**, Christian Watson **8**, Bucky Irving **6**. That's **96.67** and a **20.17**-point win, even at **-16.92 off his own average**. Matt wins ugly, which counts the same as pretty in the standings, only louder.

Mitch, meanwhile, had the tight ends. Sam LaPorta **11**, Dalton Schultz **9** — points from the one position Matt refuses to acknowledge exists — and Mitch lost by twenty anyway. If that ain't irony it's at least a close relative. Kyren Williams led him with **14**, Lamar Jackson managed **11**, Chase McLaughlin kicked **8.5**, Xavier Worthy **8**, and both defenses (Giants, Cowboys) laid goose eggs. **76.5**, **-3.5 versus his own average**, and Mitch is **0-2**, sitting **12th at 160 cumulative** (**▼1**) — dead last, and the roughest pair of losses in the winless club. Matt is **2-0** and holding **4th at 227.17**, movement **=**, which in this league is an accomplishment.

## Dan 76.5, Mike 73.67 — A Slump Ain't A Slump If You Win

Here's the game of the week for people who like their drama quiet. Dan scored **76.5**. That is **-45.75 versus his own average**. That is the **11th-best score out of twelve**. A week ago Dan hung 168 on this league; this week his aging stack showed up in a bathrobe. Travis Kelce **17**, Stefon Diggs **16**, Kenneth Walker III **12**, Daniel Jones **11**, Harrison Butker **9.5**, Aaron Rodgers **6**, Seahawks Defense **5**. Dan bet on 2022 and 2022 cashed the check late.

And he won. By **2.83**. Because Mike scored **73.67** — the lowest total in the league this week, week rank **12**, **-23.17 off his own average**. Mike got **17** from DeVonta Smith, **16** from Joe Burrow, **11** from Baker Mayfield, **9.67** from the New England Patriots Defense, and then a parade of **3**s: De'Von Achane, Justin Jefferson, D'Andre Swift, Matthew Golden. Four threes don't win ballgames. Harrison Mevis kicked **2**. If Mevis gets one more crack at it we're having a different conversation and Dan's got a different record.

Dan slides **▼1 to 2nd at 244.5** and stays **2-0**. Mike falls **▼4 to 9th at 193.67** and is **0-2**. A win is a win even when it ain't a win.

## Joe 95, Chris 88 — The Bust Of The Week Wears A Jaguars Jersey

Jared Goff threw for **32** — third-best performance in the entire league — and Chris still lost. Christian McCaffrey added **16**, Malik Willis a surprising **11**, and then Chris's famous five-tight-end collection reported for duty: Trey McBride **8**, Tyler Warren **8**, Harold Fannin Jr. **3**. A man corners the world market in tight ends and this is the dividend. And at the bottom of the box sits our official **Bust of the Week: Travis Etienne Jr., 2 points**. Two. When you come to a fork in the road, Travis, take it — just take it faster.

Joe won **95-88** on a spread-around night: Patrick Mahomes **27**, Jonathan Taylor **22**, James Cook III **17**, Jordan Love **10**, Andre Szmyt **7**, Romeo Doubs **6**. Not one of those is a fireworks show, and all of it together is a win by **7** — even at **-21.25 off Joe's own average**. Joe's **1-1** and slips **▼1 to 3rd at 232.5**. Chris drops **▼1 to 7th at 199.5**, **-11.75** off his own number. Somebody get Matt and Chris on the phone already: one's got five tight ends and two receivers, the other's got none, and they keep acting like the telephone hasn't been invented yet.

## Where It Leaves Us

Four unbeaten (Aaron **2-0**, Dan **2-0**, Matt **2-0**, Pete **2-0**), four winless (Sean, Mike, Eli, Mitch, all **0-2**), four in the middle (Joe, Bruce, Chris, Cal, all **1-1**). The league split itself in thirds and then paired up in threes.

Aaron's **280** sits on top, Dan's **244.5** behind him, and Mitch's **160** is looking up at everybody. The draft grades are already lying to us: A-grade Cal is 11th in points, A--grade Sean and B+-grade Eli are both winless, and the C+ boys — Dan and Pete — are 2-0. In theory there's no difference between the draft and the season. In practice there is.

It's early. It's so early it's late.
