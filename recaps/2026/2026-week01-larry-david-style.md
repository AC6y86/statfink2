# Week 1: A List of Grievances, Filed in Good Faith, By Somebody Who Just Wants the System to Work

So I'm sitting there Sunday, minding my own business, and the whole league decides to have a personality. Twelve people. Six games. One of them decided by 46.5 points. Who does that? You don't win by 46.5. That's not a win, that's a statement, and I don't like statements. A win should be by four. Four is polite. Four says "we both showed up." Forty-six point five says "I'd like you to think about your life."

Anyway. Here are my complaints, in order.

## Grievance #1: There was a tie at the top and nobody has a procedure for it

Caleb Williams put up **38** for Dan. Josh Allen put up **38** for Aaron. Thirty-eight and thirty-eight. The two best performances in the entire league, identical, and the league sheet lists Caleb Williams first — first! — on what basis? Alphabetical? Aesthetic? Was there a coin? Was I consulted? No. I was not consulted.

And here's the thing that gets me. Caleb Williams's 38 was the *engine* of the week's biggest score. Dan hung **168** on Joe. One sixty-eight. In a league that averaged **111.58** points this week, Dan scored 168, which is roughly "everyone else, plus a person." Behind Williams came Kenneth Walker III at **28** and David Montgomery at **27** — three of the week's top ten, all in one lineup, like the guy found a parking spot and then found two more parking spots inside it. This is the same Dan who came out of the draft with a **C+** and a roster the league decided was a nostalgia act. And what did the nostalgia act contribute? Stefon Diggs **11**. Aaron Rodgers **11**. Deebo Samuel Sr. **8**. A whole lot of "remember this guy?" — and it didn't matter, because the C+ scored 168 with two defenses, Seattle and the Chargers, sitting there at **0** and **0** like houseguests.

Joe, meanwhile, scored **137.5** and lost. **A-** draft grade, second-most points in the entire league, and he's 0-1. Patrick Mahomes **24**, Jonathan Taylor **22**, Jordan Love **22**, Isaiah Likely **22** — four men over twenty, a beautifully balanced afternoon, the kind of lineup you'd hang in a museum, and he takes an L. That's not a loss, that's a *mugging*. In any other matchup he wins and we're all congratulating him at the coffee place and I'm doing the little nod. Now I have to do the *other* nod. The sympathy nod. I hate the sympathy nod.

## Grievance #2: The kicker situation

Aaron drafted two kickers. Two! You know how many kickers scored for him? One. Cam Little, **6**. The other one's just... around. It's a spare. Who keeps a spare kicker? That's a man who's afraid of something.

And it worked anyway, because Josh Allen went for **38** and Jalen Coker — *Jalen Coker* — put up **25**, and Amon-Ra St. Brown added **19**, and Aaron beat Sean **134-109**. Sean got **33** from Derrick Henry and **28** from Jahmyr Gibbs, two top-ten performances, six backs' worth of draft capital doing exactly what six backs' worth of draft capital is supposed to do. But the QB room the draft recap called "held together with tape and prayer"? Kirk Cousins, **15**. Fifteen. Against thirty-eight. The tape held. The prayer did not. **A-** grade, 0-1 record, and a man learning that in a QB league you cannot bring a wheelbarrow of running backs to a gunfight.

Oh, and Sean started two kickers as well — Trey Smack **6.5**, Wil Lutz **2.5**. Two kickers, and between them not one Derrick Henry. He lost by 25. You do the math, and by "do the math" I mean don't, because it'll upset you.

## Grievance #3: Matt has no tight ends and it's *still working*

The whole league spent draft weekend pointing at Matt — **B** grade, nineteen players, zero tight ends. A structural void. A hole in the shape of a man. And then he goes out and beats Mike **130.5-120** on the back of Bryce Young at **35** and Christian Watson at **25**, with Jalen Hurts chipping in **21**. No tight end. Doesn't need one. It's like a guy who never learned to swim telling you he's fine because he stays out of water. Technically correct. Deeply unsettling. Saquon Barkley gave him **6** and Jason Myers gave him **4.5** and he won anyway, which is the part that'll keep Mike up at night.

Because Mike's the one I feel for, and I don't usually feel. D'Andre Swift **33**, Justin Jefferson **24**, a **120**-point Sunday — above the league average of 111.58, a good, respectable, civic-minded effort — and he loses by 10.5. DeVonta Smith gave him **3**. Cameron Dicker gave him **1**. One point! From a kicker! You know what one point is? One point is worse than zero. Zero is an honest failure. One is a man who showed up, participated, and handed you a single point like it's a mint from a dish.

Meanwhile Chris — **D+** in the draft, the lowest grade handed out, five tight ends and two receivers, a roster the recap called "broken shape" — took his tight ends out for a walk and beat Eli **111.5-91.5**. Dallas Goedert **22**. Trey McBride **14**. Tyler Warren **8**. A parade of tight ends from the man who was supposed to trade them all to Matt. Why would he trade now? He's *vindicated*. The broken shape won by 20. Although I'll note Christian McCaffrey gave him **3**, which for an "elite piece" is, let's say, a conversation for another time and a quieter room.

Eli — **B+**, the man who bet on 2028 — got a very nice 2026 out of Jaxson Dart (**24**) and Kyle Monangai (**17**), got **8** from the Pittsburgh defense, and then Puka Nacua gave him **3**. Three. From Puka Nacua. Somewhere a projection model is lying face-down on a couch.

## Grievance #4: The only close game was decided by half a point I don't understand

Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5. Margin: **7.5**. The one game all week inside a single score, and it turns on a fraction. Where does the half come from? Nobody can tell you. It just *appears*, like a cousin.

And the delicious part: Bruce is the man with, quote, "QB guilt" — he passed on nine first-round quarterbacks and then took Nix, Shough and Watson like a guy ordering the third-cheapest wine so nobody thinks he's cheap. Tyler Shough went for **30**, sixth-best performance in the league. Deshaun Watson added **11**. Bijan Robinson, **20**. The guilt paid out. **C+** draft grade, 1-0 record, and somewhere Bruce is doing a little thing with his shoulders that I don't care for.

Mitch got **26** from Trevor Lawrence and **22** from Lamar Jackson — two quarterbacks both north of twenty — and lost by seven and a half because Tee Higgins gave him **3** and both of his defenses, the Giants and the Cowboys, gave him **0**. Two defenses. Zero and zero. That's not a defense, that's a witness. Give Mitch eight points from either one and we're having an entirely different conversation, possibly a pleasant one.

## Grievance #5: Pete did 46.5 points to a man and then presumably went to brunch

Pete — **C+**, "best WR room, no backfield" — beat Cal **104.5-58**. Zay Flowers **20**, Brock Purdy **21**, C.J. Stroud **19**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba **17**. Nothing enormous, nothing top-ten, just a man handing out twenties like a wedding guest. And here's what kills me: Pete's **104.5** ranked *eighth* on the week. Eighth! Below the league average! You can post the eighth-best score in a twelve-team league and win by 46.5, which tells you everything about what happened on the other side of that scoreboard.

Fifty-eight. From the **A**. The highest draft grade in the league. The "adult build." Ashton Jeanty did his part with **25**, and then Jayden Daniels put up **10**, CeeDee Lamb put up **8**, and after that it's threes — Bhayshul Tuten **3**, Rome Odunze **3**, Tyler Allgeier **3** — the handcuff collection handcuffing nobody but Cal. Two kickers again, Jake Elliott **3.5** and Drew Stevens **2.5**, because apparently this is a league of people who hoard kickers. A **58** from an **A**. Let me ask you something: at what point do we take the grade back? Is there a mechanism? There should be a mechanism.

## The Standings, Which Are Sorted by Points and Therefore Offend Me

Dan's **1st** at **168** cumulative. Joe's **2nd** at **137.5** and he's 0-1, which is the single funniest fact in this document. Aaron **3rd** (134, 1-0), Matt **4th** (130.5, 1-0), Mike **5th** (120, 0-1), Chris **6th** (111.5, 1-0), Sean **7th** (109, 0-1), Pete **8th** (104.5, 1-0), Eli **9th** (91.5, 0-1), Bruce **10th** (91, 1-0), Mitch **11th** (83.5, 0-1), Cal **12th** (58, 0-1). Nobody moved, because there's nowhere to move *from*. Week 1. Every streak in the league is exactly one game long. Twelve men with one data point each, behaving like they've got twenty.

The gap from Dan at 168 down to Cal at 58 is the whole league stretched end to end after exactly one Sunday, which is, and I want to be precise here — pretty, pretty, pretty bad if you're Cal.

I don't like it.
