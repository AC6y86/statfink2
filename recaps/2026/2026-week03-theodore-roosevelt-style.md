# BULLY! A Message to the Peninsula Football League on the Conduct of Week Three
### Delivered from the Rear Platform of the Campaign Train by Theodore Roosevelt, Late Colonel of the Rough Riders

---

Fellow citizens of the Peninsula Football League!

I have come before you tonight not to flatter you but to tell you the truth. It was a *strenuous* week, and I say that as the highest praise I have. The easy life is for weaklings. I believe in the man in the arena: the owner who sets his nineteen, stands to it, and takes the blows. Some of you took blows this week. Some of you gave them out with a big stick. And one of you, gentlemen, fell from a height the like of which I have not seen since we went up the slope at San Juan.

The league average this week stood at 103.92 points. Remember that figure. We shall judge every man against it.

---

## I. The Charge Up Kettle Hill: Sean 141, Eli 72.5

I begin with the grandest sight on the whole field, and I am *dee-lighted* to do it.

Sean came into this week at 0-2, a man the cynics had already written off. Critics said he bet on running backs in a quarterback's league. Well! On Sunday those running backs charged like cavalry. **Jahmyr Gibbs posted 33 points, the finest single performance in the league this week,** and he did it the only way worth doing: straight at the enemy, spurs in, never looking back. Behind him Derrick Henry went for 22, the old warhorse still pulling a heavy load. And the quarterback room they said Sean had neglected answered anyway: Matthew Stafford 22, Kirk Cousins 21. Brock Bowers added 17 at tight end, and even the Tampa Bay Buccaneers Defense chipped in 5.

One hundred forty-one points. **The league high.** That is +24.33 against his own average, and it lifted Sean **three places, to third** in cumulative points with 350. He sits at 1-2, but mark me: a man who can hit 141 will not stay under .500 for long.

On the other side stood Eli, and I take no pleasure in it. Eli is 0-3 now, twelfth in the league, down two places, with 252 cumulative points. George Kittle did his duty with 22 at tight end and Dak Prescott added 16, but the line broke behind them. **Jaylen Waddle managed 2 points**, one of the week's two busts, and Omarion Hampton gave 3. Eli dressed *two* kickers, Will Reichard (7) and Nick Folk (5.5), and a man who sends kickers to do a running back's work has an injury list, not a strategy. The drafters graded Eli a B+ and called it a bet on 2028. I admire a long view, sir, but the year 2028 does not put points on the board in September. The margin was **68.5**, the biggest beating of the week.

---

## II. The Big Stick, Wielded Properly: Bruce 138.5, Cal 93.5

Speak softly and carry a big stick. Bruce carried three of them.

**Tyler Shough threw for 29. Bijan Robinson ran for 28. Bo Nix added 28 more.** Three men at 28 or better in a single lineup! Garrett Wilson put up 17 besides, Jeremiyah Love 14, Davante Adams 9, and Chris Boswell kicked 7.5. That comes to 138.5, second-best in the league, +22.67 against Bruce's own average. Bruce is **2-1, up one place to fourth**, with 347.5 cumulative points. They gave this man a C+ at the draft. I have seen a good many experts in my time, and I have seen most of them proved wrong by men who simply went and did the work.

Cal had the A grade, the finest of the draft, and he stands at 1-2. His receivers held the line: Ja'Marr Chase 14, Jordan Addison 14, CeeDee Lamb 11. The Washington Commanders Defense gave him 8. But **Kyler Murray gave 5 points at quarterback** and Ashton Jeanty 3, and in this league a man cannot win a war with his quarterback lying down. Cal scored 93.5, which is +6.5 against his own average, and he even climbed one place, **to tenth** at 261. Still, a 45-point defeat is a 45-point defeat, and I will not dress it up.

---

## III. The Narrowest Victory Since the Panama Canal Vote: Mike 104, Chris 103.5

Now, friends, we come to the fight that had me on my feet, pounding the rail of this very train.

**One half of one point.** Mike 104, Chris 103.5. The closest finish of the season.

Consider Mike's position. The injury report shows he lost Baker Mayfield to a thumb and De'Von Achane to injured reserve. A lesser man would have wept into his coffee. Mike did what I have always told young men to do: *do what you can, with what you have, where you are.* He sent in Jacoby Brissett, who answered with **27**, and Geno Smith with **26**, two quarterbacks among the week's top eight. Matthew Golden added 17 and Harrison Mevis kicked **9**. Remember that kicker.

Chris fought like a Bull Moose. Jared Goff put up 19, and **Harold Fannin Jr. matched him with 19 at tight end.** The draft critics gave Chris a D+ for five tight ends and two receivers, and Fannin made the wisdom of tight ends look better than it did in August. Christian McCaffrey and Michael Wilson each posted 14, and the Denver Broncos Defense put up 8. But look lower down that lineup, and there lies the tragedy: **Travis Etienne Jr. 3, Cam Skattebo 3, Tyler Warren 3.** With a margin of 0.5, one more point from any one of those three would have turned the result. And Mevis's 9 against Evan McPherson's 5.5 at kicker was just as decisive.

Mike is 1-2, **up one place to eighth** at 297.67, with a score +4.78 against his own average. Chris is 1-2, holds at **seventh** with 303, and has lost two straight despite scoring +2.5 against his own average. The credit belongs to the man in the arena, and Chris was in it, face marred by dust and sweat. He merely came up half a point short.

---

## IV. The Quiet March of the Unbeaten: Pete 122, Joe 99

There is an army moving quietly through this league, and nobody has sounded the bugle for it. **Pete is 3-0.**

Pete's draft grade was C+: best receiving corps, no backfield to speak of. So he won through the air, and he won with a vengeance. **Sam Darnold threw for 32, the second-best mark in the league. Brock Purdy added 29. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught for 25.** Drake London gave 12, Cooper Kupp 8, and Zay Flowers 6. The running backs, Ty Johnson and Najee Harris, gave 3 apiece, and it did not matter. The final was 122, third-best of the week, +16.28 against Pete's own average. That lifted him **two places to sixth** with 317.17 cumulative points. His record is running ahead of his point total, but the record is what counts, and Pete keeps winning.

Joe met him honorably. James Cook III led with 20, Jordan Love added 19, and Patrick Mahomes 16. But **Jonathan Taylor gave 3 and Ladd McConkey 3**, and against three Pete players at 25 or better that is bringing a pocketknife to a cavalry charge. Joe scored 99, eighth in the league this week and -11.5 against his average. He falls **two places to fifth** at 331.5 and stands 1-2. The margin was 23.

---

## V. The Old Guard Holds the Line: Dan 106.5, Mitch 94.5

Dan bet on 2022, they said. They called it nostalgia. I call it faith in proven men!

**Aaron Rodgers threw for 24**, tenth-best in the league. Kenneth Walker III ran for 19, Michael Penix Jr. added 16, Javonte Williams and Deebo Samuel Sr. posted 14 each, and **Travis Kelce contributed 11**, the old Rough Rider still in the saddle. DJ Moore gave only 3. Dan scored 106.5, -10.5 against his own average. That is not a masterpiece, but a win is a win, and Dan is **3-0, second in the league** with 351. Another C+ draft, another unbeaten record. The experts should take up a more honest trade.

Mitch is the saddest case on the whole field, and I do not say that to wound him. He scored 94.5, which is **+9.67 against his own average**. Trevor Lawrence threw for 21, Lamar Jackson 19, Tee Higgins and Terry McLaurin 14 apiece, Wan'Dale Robinson 11. Mitch is scoring, and he keeps losing. He is **0-3**, climbing one place to eleventh with 254.5. It is a hard road, Mitch, but it is the strenuous life. Far better to dare mighty things and lose by 12 than to live in the gray twilight that knows neither victory nor defeat.

---

## VI. Tumbling Down the Hill: Aaron 107, Matt 65

I have saved for last the most dramatic fall I have seen this season.

Aaron won, and won big. **Josh Allen threw for 22**, Juwan Johnson caught for 19, and Luther Burden III and Cam Ward added 11 each. Aaron scored 107, which is **-22 against his own average**. He had an off day and still won by **42**. He is **3-0, first in the league** with **387** cumulative points, holding his place. That is how a man with a real army fights: even on a bad day he routs the enemy.

And Matt. Oh, Matt. He came in 2-0 and a man of standing. He leaves with **65 points, the lowest in the league**, which is **-32.39 against his own average**, and a drop of **five places, to ninth**, at 292.17. Christian Watson and Bryce Young gave 14 each, Justin Herbert 11, Keaton Mitchell 11. After that it was a rout: Saquon Barkley 6, Tony Pollard 3, and **Denzel Boston 2**, the week's other bust. And, as this office has pointed out before, **Matt still has not one tight end on his roster.** Chris has five! I have negotiated peace between Russia and Japan, gentlemen. Surely these two men can negotiate a tight end. Matt remains 2-1 on the strength of his earlier wins, but his fortifications are crumbling.
