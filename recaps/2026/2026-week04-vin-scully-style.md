# Pull Up a Chair: PFL Week 4, As Told From the Booth

*Hi everybody, and a very pleasant good afternoon to you, wherever you may be.*

It's time for Peninsula Football League football. Pull up a chair, because we have six ballgames to talk over, and like most autumn afternoons in this league, they tell you a little something about life. About patience. About how the fellas who were told in August they'd amount to a C-plus can be sitting at 4-0 in October, while the A student is in the back of the room wondering where the lesson went.

We'll take them one at a time, the way you'd walk the base paths. No hurry. The game will wait for us.

---

## The First Inning: Joe 148.5, Sean 136

We'll start with the marquee, because it deserves to go first. The highest-scoring ballgame of the week, and there was a rhythm to it, like a pitcher's duel turned inside out where nobody could get anybody out.

Joe put up **148.5**, the best number anybody posted all week, and he got there the old-fashioned way: on the legs of his running backs. Brian Robinson Jr. carried the ball for **27**, and Jonathan Taylor added **22**. Then Romeo Doubs, a man who doesn't make many headlines, caught **19**. Patrick Mahomes and Jordan Love each threw for **16**, James Cook III had **14**, and Quinshon Judkins chipped in **11**. That's not one big swing, friends. That's a lineup that kept the line moving, one single after another, until the scoreboard operator ran out of patience. Joe finished **+28.5 against his own average**, and it moved him **up two spots to third** in cumulative points at **480**.

And here's the part that'll stay with you. Sean scored **136**. That was the **third-best score in the entire league** this week, **+14.5 against his own average**, and it lost. Chuba Hubbard ran for **25**, Kirk Cousins threw for **24**, Alvin Kamara added **16** and Brock Bowers **14**. On almost any other Sunday, that's a win going away. But the margin was **12.5**, the closest game of the week, and it went the other way.

Sean is now in a curious place, a place baseball people would recognize. He leads the whole league in cumulative points at **486**, moving **up two spots to first**, and he is **1-3**. It's a little like hitting .330 for a last-place club. The numbers say one thing and the standings say another, and all you can do is keep swinging and trust that the hits start landing in the right innings. The draft folks gave him an A-minus for betting on running backs in a quarterback league. This week the running backs came through. The schedule didn't.

## The Second Inning: Matt 137, Cal 73.5

Now, if the first inning was a duel, this one was a rout, and you might want to send the children out of the room.

Matt scored **137**, second-best of the week, and Cal scored **73.5**. The margin was **63.5**, the biggest of the week by a long way. Tetairoa McMillan caught everything thrown near him for **28**. Nico Collins added **25**. Bryce Young threw for **22**, and Ollie Gordon II ran for **17**. Matt was **+29.71 against his own average**, climbed **three spots to sixth** at **429.17**, and he's **3-1**.

And here's the thing I keep coming back to. Matt still has **zero tight ends** on his roster. Not one. It's like taking the field without a shortstop, and he keeps turning double plays anyway. Somebody ought to tell him it can't be done, but I suspect he wouldn't listen, and right now he doesn't have to.

For Cal, it was a long afternoon. CeeDee Lamb did his part with **20**, and Carnell Tate added **9**, but after that the box score reads like a quiet clubhouse: Kyler Murray **6**, Ashton Jeanty **6**, Rome Odunze **6**, and both defenses at **0**. Cal was **-10.13 against his own average**, slid **two spots to twelfth** at **334.5**, and he's **1-3** on an **L2** streak. You'll remember the draft graders handed Cal an **A**, the best mark in the class. Four weeks in, he's last in points. Baseball's a humbling game, and so is this one. Ask anybody who ever got a big contract in the winter.

## The Third Inning: Eli 128, Bruce 92.5

Well, it took a while, but Eli has a win, and you could almost hear the sigh of relief from the dugout.

Eli scored **128**, fourth-best in the week and **+33 against his own average**, the biggest jump over his own norm of anybody in the league. He beat Bruce by **35.5**. Kyle Monangai ran for **25**, Jameis Winston threw for **24**, and Dak Prescott and Puka Nacua had **17** apiece. George Kittle added **11**, and Will Reichard kicked his way to **10**. After an **0-3** start, Eli is **1-3**, he moved **up three spots to ninth** at **380**, and his streak reads **W1** for the first time all season. The draft people said Eli was building for 2028. It turns out 2026 had a nice afternoon in it after all.

Bruce had two backs who did their jobs. Bijan Robinson ran for **25** and Emanuel Wilson added **22**. But the rest of the lineup went quiet: Tyler Shough and Deshaun Watson at **14** each, Aaron Jones Sr. **6**, and then a string of threes. Bruce finished **92.5**, **-17.5 against his own average**, ninth in the week, and slipped **one spot to fifth** at **440**. He's **2-2**, and his last five read **WLWL**, a man who can't quite find two good days in a row.

## The Fourth Inning: Dan 113, Aaron 93.5

Now let's talk about Dan. The draft folks gave him a **C-plus** and said he had bet on 2022. Well, somebody forgot to tell 2022 that it was over.

Aaron Rodgers threw for **26**. Kenneth Walker III ran for **28**, tied for the second-best individual number in the league, and Javonte Williams ran for **27**. Dan put up **113**, actually **-3 against his own average**, and still beat Aaron by **19.5**. He's **4-0** with a **W4** streak, and the league's grading curve is beginning to look like something you'd find at the bottom of a bird cage. He did slip **two spots to fourth** in points at **464**, which tells you he's winning on timing as much as volume. But they don't hand out banners for cumulative points.

Aaron had a day to forget. Josh Allen threw for **22** and Malik Nabers caught **17**, but Aaron was **-26.63 against his own average**, the steepest drop of anyone. Amon-Ra St. Brown managed only **6**, Chase Brown **3**. Aaron finished **93.5**, eighth in the week, dropped **one spot to second** at **480.5**, and saw his three-game winning run end. He's **3-1**, streak **L1**. Still a fine team. Just not on this day.

## The Fifth Inning: Pete 95.5, Mike 64

And here is the other C-plus that won't go away. Pete scored only **95.5**, seventh in the week and **-7.67 against his own average**, and he won by **31.5**, because sometimes the other fella just can't get anything going.

C.J. Stroud threw for **22**, Zay Flowers caught **17**, and Brock Purdy added **16**. Both of Pete's defenses, Minnesota and Chicago, came through with **5** apiece, which is more than nearly every other defense in this league managed all week. Pete's **4-0** with a **W4** streak, sitting seventh in points at **412.67** after slipping **one spot**. The draft recap said he had the best receiving room and no backfield, and the backfield did its best to prove it: **Najee Harris finished with 2 points**, the week's lone bust among the skill players. Rhamondre Stevenson managed **10**. Pete won anyway, the way a good club wins a game its cleanup hitter goes 0-for-4.

Mike, I'm sorry to say, had the low score in the league at **64**, **-26.42 against his own average**. And the cruel thing is that Joe Burrow played wonderfully, throwing for **28**, tied for the second-best individual score of the week. But after Burrow, Jacoby Brissett had **10**, Tucker Kraft and Matthew Golden **8** each, and D'Andre Swift **3**. Both defenses gave him **0**. Mike dropped **two spots to tenth** at **361.67** and he's **1-3**. One great hitter can't win alone if nobody's on base.

## The Sixth Inning: Mitch 105, Chris 84.5

And we close with a story about the finest single performance of the afternoon, and how it wasn't nearly enough.

**Drake Maye threw for 29 points.** That was the top individual score in the entire Peninsula Football League this week, better than Walker, better than Burrow, better than McMillan. He stood in there like a young Koufax with all the stuff in the world. Jared Goff added **20** behind him. And Chris lost.

Because Mitch had Kyren Williams running for **25**, Lamar Jackson throwing for **16**, Sam LaPorta catching **14**, and Tee Higgins adding **12**. Even the New York Giants defense chipped in **8**. Mitch scored **105**, **+15.13 against his own average**, and won by **20.5** for his first victory of the season. He's still **1-3** and still **eleventh** in points at **359.5**, unchanged, but a man who's been carrying an 0-fer gets to enjoy the first one.

Chris finished **84.5**, tenth in the week, **-12.38 against his own average**, and slipped **one spot to eighth** at **387.5**. Christian McCaffrey had **11**, Harold Fannin Jr. **8**, and after that it got lean. The draft people gave Chris a **D-plus**, and you'll recall that this is the roster with **five tight ends and two receivers**, which is a bit like carrying five catchers. Chris is **1-3** with an **L3** streak, and his last five read **WLLL**. He had the best player on the field this week, and it still wasn't enough.
