# Happy Little Accidents: The Joy of Week 1

Well hello there, friend. Come on in, pull up a chair. I've got a fresh canvas
here, twelve empty ones actually, and today we're gonna start a whole new
season together. Isn't that exciting? Nothing but liquid white and possibility.
No records, no regrets, no reason anybody can't be a champion. Just you, me, and
a two-inch brush.

Let's dip into a little titanium white and see what happens.

---

## The Almighty Mountain: Dan 168, Joe 137.5

Every painting needs one big shape that says *look at me*, and this week Dan
painted the mountain. One hundred sixty-eight points. That's the tallest peak on
the whole canvas, and he built it with a palette knife and no apologies.

Caleb Williams came in at **38 points** — co-leader of the entire league this
week — and behind him Dan stacked two more happy little foothills: Kenneth
Walker III at **28** and David Montgomery at **27**. Three players in the week's
top ten from one roster. Now, the draft folks handed Dan a **C+** and said he
"bet on 2022," and you know what? The old paint still flows. Stefon Diggs
**11**, Aaron Rodgers **11**, Deebo Samuel Sr. **8**. There's no such thing as a
tube of color that's too old, friend. You just gotta warm it in your hands.

And here's the part that'll break your heart a little. Joe scored **137.5** —
the second-most points in the entire league — and lost by **30.5**. Patrick
Mahomes **24**, Jonathan Taylor **22**, Jordan Love **22**, Isaiah Likely **22**,
Ladd McConkey **14**. That's four different players at twenty-plus. In any other
game on this canvas, Joe walks away smiling. The **A-** draft grade wasn't
wrong. The schedule just was. Sometimes you lay down a beautiful stroke and the
brush next to yours lays down a *slightly more* beautiful one. That's not a
mistake. That's just where the light fell.

## Two Suns in One Sky: Aaron 134, Sean 109

You can't have two light sources in a landscape, they'll tell you. Week 1 said
*watch me*.

Josh Allen put up **38 points** for Aaron — dead even with Caleb Williams atop
the league — and Derrick Henry answered with **33** for Sean, with Jahmyr Gibbs
right behind at **28**. That's a shootout, friend. That's two brushes beating
the devil out of the same canvas.

But arithmetic is a quiet thing. Aaron had depth underneath the big shape: Jalen
Coker **25**, Amon-Ra St. Brown **19**, Chris Olave **12**, Chase Brown **11**,
Juwan Johnson **11**, Dalton Kincaid **9**. Aaron **134**, Sean **109**. And oh,
the draft chatter about Aaron drafting two kickers? Only Cam Little's **6** made
the lineup. Sometimes you buy two fan brushes and only ever use the one. No harm
in it.

Sean, graded **A-**, bet on running backs in a quarterback league, and the backs
absolutely showed up. But Kirk Cousins came in at **15**, and that QB room the
draft recap called "held together with tape and prayer" needed a little more
prayer. Only eight of Sean's players put color on the canvas at all.

## The Nail-Biter: Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5

Now this one — this one I painted slowly.

**91 to 83.5.** A margin of **7.5**. The only game in the whole league decided
by less than ten points, and the closest thing we've got to drama this week.

Bruce's brush was Tyler Shough, of all people, at **30 points**. Remember the
draft note — Bruce passed on nine first-round quarterbacks and then took
Nix/Shough/Watson, and folks called it "QB guilt"? Well, Shough dropped **30**
and Deshaun Watson added **11**, and Bruce's **C+** roster is sitting at 1-0.
Bijan Robinson chipped in **20**, Aaron Jones Sr. and Jeremiyah Love **8**
apiece, Garrett Wilson **6**, Chris Boswell **5**.

Mitch had the quarterbacks — Trevor Lawrence **26** (tenth-best score in the
league) and Lamar Jackson **22** — plus Breece Hall at **17**. But after Kyren
Williams' **8** and Tyler Bass' **7.5**, the paint ran thin. Tee Higgins **3**.
Two defenses at zero. When the margin is 7.5, friend, every single brushstroke
you *didn't* make shows up in the finished piece. One more Higgins catch. One
more Bass kick. That's your whole season, right there in a thimble.

## Detail Work: Matt 130.5, Mike 120

The draft recap told us Matt drafted nineteen players and **zero tight ends**.
Well, he went out and scored **130.5** anyway, and here's how: Bryce Young
**35** — third-best score in the league — Christian Watson **25**, Jalen Hurts
**21**, Nico Collins **14**, Denzel Boston **11**. You don't need every color in
the box. You just need the ones you've got to sing.

Mike put **120** on the board and it wasn't enough by **10.5**. D'Andre Swift
went for **33** (tied for fourth-best in the league), Justin Jefferson **24**,
and then the strangest little cluster of threes and fourteens you ever saw: Joe
Burrow **14**, Baker Mayfield **14**, Parker Washington **14**, Jordan Mason
**11**. And Cameron Dicker, bless him, kicked in **1**. One point. If a kicker
can be a happy little accident, that's him. Mike's **B-** build produced the
fifth-most points in the league and a loss. That happens.

## Brush Cleaning: Chris 111.5, Eli 91.5

Chris got a **D+** at the draft for owning five tight ends and two receivers.
"Broken shape," they called it. Friend, let me tell you what broken shape looks
like when it wins: Dallas Goedert **22**, Trey McBride **14**, Tyler Warren
**8**. Three tight ends, all of them contributing, plus Jared Goff **16**, Cam
Skattebo **14**, Malik Willis **14**, Evan McPherson **9.5**, and the 49ers
defense for **5**. Chris **111.5**, and he's 1-0. There are no mistakes in this
league — only unusual compositions.

Eli, graded **B+** and "betting on 2028," got a lovely glimpse of the future:
Jaxson Dart **24**, Kyle Monangai **17**, Dak Prescott **16**, the Steelers
defense **8**. But **91.5** doesn't hang in a gallery yet. The young trees are
planted. They just need a few more weeks of sunshine.

## The Palette Knife: Pete 104.5, Cal 58

And then there's the one where somebody scraped the whole canvas clean.

Pete **104.5**, Cal **58**. A margin of **46.5** — the biggest gap of the week
by a country mile. Pete, drafted **C+** with "the best WR room, no backfield,"
let the receivers do the work: Zay Flowers **20**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba **17**,
Rashee Rice **8**, with Brock Purdy **21** and C.J. Stroud **19** throwing the
paint and Jacory Croskey-Merritt sneaking in **11** from that supposedly empty
backfield.

Cal got the league's only **A** draft grade. Cal scored **58**. Ashton Jeanty
was magnificent at **25** — and then the canvas went awful quiet. Jayden Daniels
**10**. CeeDee Lamb **8**. Then threes: Bhayshul Tuten, Rome Odunze, Tyler
Allgeier. Two kickers for **3.5** and **2.5**. Both defenses at zero. The league
averaged **111.58** points this week, friend. Cal painted about half a picture.

But here's the thing I want you to hear, Cal, and I mean this: it's just one
canvas. You can't have light without dark. You can't appreciate the mountain
without the valley. And the very best part of week one is that there are
eleven more weeks of regular season sitting right there, wet and ready.

## Where the Easels Stand

Through one week, the standings sort by cumulative points, and it's a mirror of
the scoreboard: **Dan 1st at 168**, **Joe 2nd at 137.5** despite an 0-1 start,
**Aaron 3rd at 134**, **Matt 4th at 130.5**, **Mike 5th at 120** (also 0-1),
**Chris 6th at 111.5**, **Sean 7th at 109**, **Pete 8th at 104.5**, **Eli 9th at
91.5**, **Bruce 10th at 91**, **Mitch 11th at 83.5**, and **Cal 12th at 58**.
No movement arrows yet — you can't move on your first stroke.

Six teams carry a W1 streak: Dan, Aaron, Matt, Chris, Pete, Bruce. Six carry an
L1: Joe, Mike, Sean, Eli, Mitch, Cal. Every matchup was a first meeting. Nobody
owes anybody anything yet. Give it time.
