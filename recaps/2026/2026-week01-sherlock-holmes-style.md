# The Adventure of the Opening Gambit

*From the notebooks of John H. Watson, M.D. — Peninsula Football League, Week the First, 2026*

I found Holmes at the window of 221B, violin idle across his knee, the twelve
box scores of the Peninsula Football League spread upon the carpet like the
exhibits of a coroner's inquest.

"You have read the returns, Watson."

"I have. A dozen scores, six verdicts. Rather ordinary, I should think, for an
opening week."

"Ordinary!" He wheeled about. "You see, but you do not observe. The league
averaged **111.58** points. The draft board — that solemn document of last
month, with its confident letter grades — has already been exposed as a
forgery. Data, Watson! Data, data, data. I cannot make bricks without clay. Let
us proceed in order."

---

## Exhibit I — The Man Who Bet On 2022 (Dan 168, Joe 137.5)

"Begin with the highest-scoring game in the league, for the loudest crime is
usually the simplest. Dan, graded **C+**, a man the draft room accused of
betting on the year 2022, produced **168** points — the most in the league —
and beat Joe **168 - 137.5**.

"Observe the instrument. **Caleb Williams, 38**. Behind him **Kenneth Walker
III, 28**, and **David Montgomery, 27** — three men from one roster in the
week's top ten, a concentration of force no opponent may answer by degrees.
**DJ Moore 17**, **Javonte Williams 16**, then the relics themselves: **Stefon
Diggs 11**, **Aaron Rodgers 11**, **Deebo Samuel Sr. 8**, **Cairo Santos 6**.
The oldest core in the league, Watson, and it walked off the field first in the
table."

"And Joe? Graded **A-**."

"Joe is the tragedy of the week and you must not let it pass unremarked. He
scored **137.5** — the *second-highest total of all twelve teams* — and lost.
**Patrick Mahomes 24**, **Jonathan Taylor 22**, **Jordan Love 22**, **Isaiah
Likely 22**, **Ladd McConkey 14**, **Demarcus Robinson 11**, **Mike Evans 8**,
**Tyler Loop 6.5**, even the **Kansas City Chiefs Defense** for **5**. Against
any other man in the league he wins handsomely. Against Dan he is merely a
well-dressed corpse; the margin was **30.5**. The **A-** build sits at
**0-1**; the **C+** build sits alone atop the table. When you have eliminated
the impossible, Watson, whatever remains — however insulting to the draft
grader — must be the truth."

---

## Exhibit II — The Shootout at Thirty-Eight (Aaron 134, Sean 109)

"Two men fired at each other with the heaviest weapons available and only one
walked away. **Josh Allen, 38** for Aaron — level with Caleb Williams for the
finest single performance of the week. Sean answered with **Derrick Henry,
33**, and **Jahmyr Gibbs, 28**, and still lost **134 - 109**.

"The margin of **25** was not made by the stars; it was made by the supporting
cast. Mark **Jalen Coker, 25** — a name to remember — then **Amon-Ra St. Brown
19**, **Chris Olave 12**, **Chase Brown 11**, **Juwan Johnson 11**, **Dalton
Kincaid 9**, **Cam Little 6**, **Blake Corum 3**. Aaron's **B+** roster scored
at nine separate addresses. Sean's **A-** roster — the man who bet on running
backs in a quarterback league — filled the remainder of its ledger with **Chuba
Hubbard 16**, **Kirk Cousins 15**, the **Tampa Bay Buccaneers Defense 8**,
**Trey Smack 6.5**, **Wil Lutz 2.5**. The quarterback room held together with
tape and prayer produced fifteen. The tape held. The prayer did not."

"And Aaron drafted two kickers."

"He did, and one of them gave him **6**. I shall not mock a man whose
arithmetic works."

---

## Exhibit III — The Curious Incident of the Tight End (Matt 130.5, Mike 120)

"Here is the singular feature of the case, Watson. Matt drafted nineteen
players and not one tight end."

"But the tight end did nothing in this game."

"*That* was the curious incident. He won anyway, **130.5 - 120**, by **10.5**,
on the strength of **Bryce Young, 35** — third-best mark in the league —
**Christian Watson, 25**, **Jalen Hurts 21**, **Nico Collins 14**, **Denzel
Boston 11**, **Bucky Irving 8**, **Saquon Barkley 6**, **Tetairoa McMillan
6**, **Jason Myers 4.5**. A **B** grade, a missing position, and a win. Chris,
who owns five tight ends, will want to place his call before Matt discovers he
does not need one.

"Mike, graded **B-**, had a finer single performance in defeat than Matt had in
victory: **D'Andre Swift, 33**, fourth in the league, with **Justin Jefferson
24** beside him. But observe the seam — **Joe Burrow 14**, **Baker Mayfield
14**, **Parker Washington 14**, **Jordan Mason 11**, **Matthew Golden 6**,
**DeVonta Smith 3**, and **Cameron Dicker 1**. One point from the kicker,
Watson, in a game lost by **10.5**. Give Mike an ordinary afternoon from that
foot and the margin narrows toward a whisper — though I concede even then he
does not quite arrive. The case is not a single wound but a slow bleeding."

---

## Exhibit IV — The Seven-and-a-Half-Point Problem (Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5)

"The only game in the league decided inside a single score. Bruce **91**, Mitch
**83.5**, margin **7.5**.

"You will recall the accusation of *quarterback guilt* — that Bruce passed on
nine first-round quarterbacks and then took Nix, Shough and Watson as a man
takes medicine. Well: **Tyler Shough, 30**, sixth-best performance in the
league, and **Deshaun Watson, 11** behind him. Add **Bijan Robinson, 20**,
**Jeremiyah Love 8**, **Aaron Jones Sr. 8**, **Garrett Wilson 6**, **Chris
Boswell 5**, **Emeka Egbuka 3**. The C+ man wins again.

"Mitch had the better quarterback room on paper and played both — **Trevor
Lawrence, 26**, tenth in the league, and **Lamar Jackson, 22** — and still fell
short, with **Breece Hall 17**, **Kyren Williams 8**, **Tyler Bass 7.5**, **Tee
Higgins 3**. Seven and a half points, Watson. A field goal and a breath. That
is a three-pipe problem and I shall still be turning it over come Sunday."

---

## Exhibit V — The Broken Shape That Worked (Chris 111.5, Eli 91.5)

"Chris was graded **D+** — the lowest mark issued — for owning five tight ends
and two receivers. A broken shape, they said. Observe what the broken shape
did: **Dallas Goedert 22**, **Trey McBride 14**, **Tyler Warren 8** — three
tight ends in one scoring lineup — and a **111.5 - 91.5** verdict by **20**.
Add **Jared Goff 16**, **Cam Skattebo 14**, **Malik Willis 14**, **Evan
McPherson 9.5**, the **San Francisco 49ers Defense 5**, and three men at **3**
apiece: **Christian McCaffrey**, **Jadarian Price**, **Michael Wilson**.

"Eli, the man who bet on 2028, got a glimpse of the future and not enough of
the present: **Jaxson Dart 24**, **Kyle Monangai 17**, **Dak Prescott 16**,
**Omarion Hampton 8**, **Jakobi Meyers 8**, the **Pittsburgh Steelers Defense
8**, **Nick Folk 4.5**, **Puka Nacua 3**, **Alec Pierce 3**. Ninth in the table
at **91.5**. The lottery tickets are printed; the drawing is later."

---

## Exhibit VI — The A-Grade Catastrophe (Pete 104.5, Cal 58)

"And now the body in the study. Cal — graded **A**, the highest mark in the
league, the *adult build*, stars early and starters late — scored **58**. Not
merely last: **58**, against a league average of **111.58**.

"His entire ledger: **Ashton Jeanty 25**, **Jayden Daniels 10**, **CeeDee Lamb
8**, **Jake Elliott 3.5**, **Bhayshul Tuten 3**, **Rome Odunze 3**, **Tyler
Allgeier 3**, **Drew Stevens 2.5**, and two defenses at **0**. One man reached
twenty. The handcuff bench, you will note, handcuffed no one but its owner.

"Pete — **C+**, best receiving room, no backfield — required nothing exotic:
**Brock Purdy 21**, **Zay Flowers 20**, **C.J. Stroud 19**, **Jaxon
Smith-Njigba 17**, **Jacory Croskey-Merritt 11**, **Rashee Rice 8**, **Eddy
Pineiro 5.5**, **Rhamondre Stevenson 3**. Total: **104.5** — *eighth* in the
league this week — and it produced the largest margin of the round, **46.5**.
That is the most damning number in the file, Watson. Pete won by more than any
man alive while scoring beneath the league average. One does not require a
cannon to shoot a sitting duck."

---

## Holmes's Summation — The Table After One

"The ledger, ranked by cumulative points, every man's movement marked with a
dash, for there is nothing yet to move from.

"**Dan, 1-0, 168**. **Joe, 0-1, 137.5** — second in points, last in mood.
**Aaron, 1-0, 134**. **Matt, 1-0, 130.5**. **Mike, 0-1, 120**. **Chris, 1-0,
111.5**. **Sean, 0-1, 109**. **Pete, 1-0, 104.5**. **Eli, 0-1, 91.5**.
**Bruce, 1-0, 91**. **Mitch, 0-1, 83.5**. **Cal, 0-1, 58**.

"Note the disorder, Watson. Joe stands second in the table and owns a defeat.
Pete stands eighth and owns a victory. Bruce is tenth in points and unbeaten;
Mike is fifth in points and beaten. Every streak in the league is precisely one
game long; every man's last five reads a single letter. Every matchup was a
first meeting, so no grudge has yet been entered into evidence — but grudges,
like debts, accrue.

"And the grades? **C+ Dan** leads the league in points. **C+ Pete** and **C+
Bruce** won. **D+ Chris** won. **A Cal** lost by **46.5**, and **A- Joe** lost
while scoring **137.5**. Never theorise before you have data. It biases the
judgment — and one week of it has already made a fool of an entire draft
board."

He took up the violin at last.

"The game, my dear Watson, is afoot."
