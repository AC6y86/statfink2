# The State of the League: Remarks on Week 4 of the Peninsula Football League

*As prepared for delivery.*

---

Commissioner, members of the Peninsula Football League, my fellow owners.

Thank you. Please, have a seat. Some of you are sitting a little lower in the standings than you'd like, and look, I understand. I've had weeks where the polls were not kind. But we're not here tonight to dwell on what went wrong. We're here to talk about where we are, and where we go from here.

So let me be clear. The state of our league is competitive. Four weeks in, it is messy, it is a little unfair, and it is ours.

---

## I. Hope, Measured in Points

I want to start with a story about an owner named Joe.

A week ago, Joe was 1-2. Folks said his roster had peaked in the draft room. This week Joe put up **148.5 points**, the highest score in the league and **+28.5** over his own average, and moved **up two spots to third** in cumulative points at **480**.

How did he do it? Not with one hero, but with a coalition. **Brian Robinson Jr.** ran for **27**. **Jonathan Taylor** added **22**. **Romeo Doubs**, who I don't think anybody built a campaign around, gave him **19**. **Patrick Mahomes** and **Jordan Love** chipped in **16** apiece, and **James Cook III** added **14**. That is not a superstar carrying the team. That is a lot of people doing their jobs, and that's how anything worth doing gets done.

He needed every bit of it, because across the aisle was **Sean**, who scored **136**, the third-best total of the week and **+14.5** over his own average, and lost **148.5-136**. **Chuba Hubbard** put up **25**. **Kirk Cousins** added **24**. **Alvin Kamara** had **16** and **Brock Bowers** had **14**. On a normal Sunday, that wins.

No game this week finished inside ten points. But at **12.5**, this was the closest one we had, and it was also the highest-scoring game of the week. Our draft analysts said Sean "bet on RBs in a QB league," and the backs held up their end. The math just didn't go his way.

I want to name the paradox of Sean's season plainly. Sean is now **first in the league in cumulative points, at 486**, up two spots, and he is **1-3**. In a fairer world, points would turn into wins. But this is a head-to-head league, and you play the opponent the schedule hands you. Sean, don't let the setbacks define you. Those points are going to start counting.

---

## II. The Unlikely Majority: Dan and Pete

I've always been suspicious of conventional wisdom, and this season has given me good reason.

Back in the draft, the experts handed out grades. **Dan got a C+. Pete got a C+.** Four weeks later, they are the **only two unbeaten teams in the league, both 4-0 on a W4 streak.** I'm not saying grades don't matter. I'm saying the people in the room don't always know what the people on the field are about to do.

**Dan beat Aaron, 113-93.5**, a margin of **19.5**. The analysts said Dan "bet on 2022," and 2022 showed up. **Kenneth Walker III** put up **28**, tied for the second-best individual score in the league. **Javonte Williams** added **27**. And **Aaron Rodgers**, a man who has heard more retirement speculation than any public servant should, threw for **26**. **Michael Penix Jr.** added **11** and **Cairo Santos** kicked **7**. Dan's **113** was actually **-3** under his own average, so he didn't need his best stuff. Only 4-0 teams get to say that.

Aaron had a hard week, and I want to be fair to him. **Josh Allen** gave him **22**, **Malik Nabers** **17**, and **Cam Ward** **13**. But **Amon-Ra St. Brown** managed **6** and **Chase Brown** **3**, and **93.5** was **-26.63** under Aaron's average. Aaron is still **3-1** and **second in cumulative points at 480.5**, down one spot. Dan sits **fourth at 464**, down two. In this league, an unbeaten record and a points title don't always live in the same house.

**Pete beat Mike, 95.5-64**, a **31.5**-point margin. Pete's **95.5** was only **seventh** in the week, **-7.67** below his own average, and he still won by more than thirty. The analysts said Pete had the "best WR room, no backfield," and this week proved them right on both counts. **C.J. Stroud** threw for **22**, **Zay Flowers** caught for **17**, and **Brock Purdy** added **16**. The **Minnesota Vikings Defense** and the **Chicago Bears Defense** posted **5** each, and in a week when most defenses in this league put up zeros, that matters.

Then there's the backfield. I believe in accountability, so I'll say it: **Najee Harris put up 2 points.** He was this week's official bust. When your running back scores less than a field goal, it's time for a frank conversation. Pete won anyway. He's **4-0**, but he's **seventh in cumulative points at 412.67**, down one. The record says contender. The points total says he should keep an eye on things.

Mike's week is going to stay with me. **Joe Burrow** scored **28**, also tied for the second-best individual total in the league, and Mike's team scored **64**, the **lowest in the league** and **-26.42** below his average. Behind Burrow you had **Jacoby Brissett** at **10**, **Tucker Kraft** and **Matthew Golden** at **8** each, and **D'Andre Swift** at **3**. Burrow carried that roster as far as one man can. Mike drops **two spots to tenth**, at **361.67**.

---

## III. A Victory Long Deferred

Now let me talk about two owners who finally got a win.

**Eli beat Bruce, 128-92.5**, by **35.5**, after starting **0-3**. The analysts said Eli "bet on 2028," and I respect someone who plays the long game. Tonight I'm happy to report that the future arrived a couple of years early. **Kyle Monangai** ran for **25**. **Jameis Winston**, who will always try the throw, gave him **24**. **Dak Prescott** and **Puka Nacua** added **17** each, **George Kittle** **11**, and **Will Reichard** kicked **10**. That was **+33** over Eli's own average, the biggest jump of anyone this week, and it lifted him **three spots, to ninth**, at **380**.

Bruce got real production at the top. **Bijan Robinson** scored **25** and **Emanuel Wilson** added **22**. But **Tyler Shough** and **Deshaun Watson** stopped at **14** apiece, and the rest went quiet: **Aaron Jones Sr.** **6**, then **Jeremiyah Love**, **Colston Loveland** and **KC Concepcion** at **3** each. **92.5** was **-17.5** under his average. Bruce is **2-2**, **fifth at 440**, down one. That's a setback, not a crisis.

**Mitch beat Chris, 105-84.5**, by **20.5**, his first win of the year after an **LLLW** run. **Kyren Williams** led the way with **25**. **Lamar Jackson** added **16**, **Sam LaPorta** **14**, **Tee Higgins** **12**, and the **New York Giants Defense** put up **8**, the best defensive score in the league. That's **+15.13** over Mitch's average. He's still **eleventh at 359.5**, unchanged. But the first win is always the hardest.

Chris's story is harder to tell. **Drake Maye scored 29 points, the best individual performance in the entire league this week**, and Chris still lost. **Jared Goff** added **20** and **Christian McCaffrey** **11**. After that, **Harold Fannin Jr.** had **8**, **Michael Wilson** **6**, and **Cam Skattebo** **3**. The analysts gave Chris a **D+** and pointed to a roster with five tight ends and two receivers. They weren't wrong about the problem. Chris is **1-3** on an **L3** streak, **eighth at 387.5**, down one. Chris, you've found your quarterback. Now build around him.

---

## IV. The Blowout, and the Grade That Hasn't Held Up

**Matt beat Cal, 137-73.5.** The margin was **63.5**, the largest of the week.

Matt scored the **second-highest total in the league**, **+29.71** over his own average. **Tetairoa McMillan** had **28**. **Nico Collins** added **25**. **Bryce Young** threw for **22**, **Ollie Gordon II** ran for **17**, **Justin Herbert** added **11**, and **Jake Bates** kicked **9**. Matt jumps **three spots, to sixth**, at **429.17**, and he's **3-1**, all **with zero tight ends on his roster.** I don't recommend it as a strategy, but I can't argue with the result.

And then there's Cal. Cal got the **highest grade in the draft, an A.** Four weeks later Cal is **1-3**, scored **73.5** (**-10.13** under his average), and dropped **two spots to twelfth, last in cumulative points at 334.5.** **CeeDee Lamb** did his job with **20**. After that it was **Carnell Tate** **9**, **Tyler Allgeier** **8**, **Ashton Jeanty** and **Kyler Murray** **6** each, and both defenses at zero. Cal, draft grades are promises, and promises have to be kept on Sundays. You've still got time.

## V. Where We Stand

Through four weeks:

1. **Sean**, 486, 1-3, up two
2. **Aaron**, 480.5, 3-1, down one
3. **Joe**, 480, 2-2, up two
4. **Dan**, 464, 4-0, down two
5. **Bruce**, 440, 2-2, down one
6. **Matt**, 429.17, 3-1, up three
7. **Pete**, 412.67, 4-0, down one
8. **Chris**, 387.5, 1-3, down one
9. **Eli**, 380, 1-3, up three
10. **Mike**, 361.67, 1-3, down two
11. **Mitch**, 359.5, 1-3, unchanged
12. **Cal**, 334.5, 1-3, down two

League average this week: **105.92**.

Look at the top three: **486. 480.5. 480.** That race is wide open. And the two unbeaten teams are fourth and seventh in points. The standings and the scoreboard aren't telling the same story yet.

This season rewards the people who keep showing up. Dan and Pete are proving the grades wrong. Sean is waiting for his luck to catch up with his scoring. Joe, Matt, Eli and Mitch are on the way up. Cal and Mike are at the bottom, for now.

To the six of you sitting at 1-3, we're four weeks into twelve. There's still time to turn this around. Yes, you can.

Thank you. God bless you, and God bless the Peninsula Football League.
