# The Peninsula: Life Returns — Week 1, 2026

*Whispered, from a hide at the edge of the standings.*

For eight long months, the Peninsula lay dormant. The great scoreboard stood
cold and empty. Twelve creatures — some ancient, some barely weaned — waited
out the drought on the strength of a single April afternoon, the draft, where
each built the body it would have to live in.

Now the rains have come. And with them, Week 1.

Observe. The herd moves. Twelve teams, six confrontations, and a league
average of **111.58 points** scattered across the plain like carrion. Some
feasted. One, as we shall see, did not eat at all.

---

## The Alpha: Dan, and the Old Bulls Who Would Not Lie Down

Here, on the highest ground, stands **Dan**. He entered this season carrying a
draft grade of **C+** — the scouts had written him off as a relic, a creature
who "bet on 2022," the oldest core in the league. Nature, it seems, had not
read the scouting report.

Dan's **168** points are the most any animal took in a single week, and he
took them from **Joe**, who scored **137.5** — a total that would have won
four of the other five games on this plain. It is the cruellest arithmetic in
the wild: to run faster than almost everyone, and still be eaten. Joe, graded
**A-**, one of the two "adult" builds in the league, got **24** from Patrick
Mahomes, **22** apiece from Jonathan Taylor, Jordan Love, and Isaiah Likely,
**14** from Ladd McConkey — a beautifully balanced organism — and sits
**0-1** anyway, second in cumulative points at **137.5**.

Because Dan had the backfield. **Caleb Williams, 38.** **Kenneth Walker III,
28.** **David Montgomery, 27.** Three of the week's ten best performances,
all grazing in one herd, with **17** from DJ Moore and **16** from Javonte
Williams for good measure. Even the fossils stirred: Stefon Diggs **11**,
Aaron Rodgers **11**, Deebo Samuel Sr. **8**. The old bulls, thought to be
dying, begin the season **1-0** and first in cumulative points at **168**,
having won the week's highest-scoring game by **30.5**.

A remarkable creature. We shall watch to see how long the joints hold.

---

## The Shootout: Aaron and Sean Display, and Only One Survives

Elsewhere on the savanna, two males engaged in the most spectacular display of
the week. **Aaron 134, Sean 109.**

**Josh Allen** produced **38 points** — tied for the finest individual
performance anywhere in the league, matching Caleb Williams blow for blow
across a hundred miles of open country. Behind him, Aaron's flock: **Jalen
Coker 25**, **Amon-Ra St. Brown 19**, **Chris Olave 12**, and a pair of tight
ends, Juwan Johnson **11** and Dalton Kincaid **9**.

Sean, graded **A-**, had bet everything on running backs in a quarterback's
league — and for one glorious afternoon the bet nearly paid. **Derrick Henry,
33.** **Jahmyr Gibbs, 28.** **Chuba Hubbard, 16.** Three sets of legs, all
thundering. But the quarterback room the scouts called "held together with
tape and prayer" produced exactly **15** from Kirk Cousins, and in a league
where the two best scores of the week were both quarterbacks, that is the
difference between the kill and the carcass. Sean falls to **0-1**, seventh in
cumulative points at **109**. Aaron rises to **1-0** and third at **134**.

Note, for the record, that Aaron drafted two kickers. Only Cam Little
(**6**) was required today.

---

## The Blowout: Pete and the Unfortunate Cal

Now we come to the most difficult footage of the week. Viewers of a sensitive
disposition may wish to look away.

**Cal** arrived with the season's only **A** grade — stars early, starters
late, a bench of handcuffs. He scored **58 points**, the lowest total on the
Peninsula, and sits twelfth. **Ashton Jeanty** ran for **25** of it,
essentially alone. Behind him: Jayden Daniels **10**, CeeDee Lamb **8**, and
then a long, quiet plain of threes — Bhayshul Tuten **3**, Rome Odunze **3**,
Tyler Allgeier **3**.

**Pete**, meanwhile — graded **C+**, best receiver room, no backfield — simply
walked over and took what he wanted. **104.5 to 58**, a margin of **46.5**,
the largest gap of the week. Brock Purdy **21**, Zay Flowers **20**, C.J.
Stroud **19**, Jaxon Smith-Njigba **17**, Jacory Croskey-Merritt **11**. Pete
did not need a spectacular day — his **104.5** sits eighth in the league this
week. He needed only to be present, and he was.

The A-grade animal is last. The C+ animal is 1-0. The wild does not care what
was written about it in April.

---

## The Nail-Biter: Bruce Survives by a Whisker

Only one contest all week came within a single score, and it was the least
glamorous of the lot. **Bruce 91, Mitch 83.5. A margin of 7.5.**

Bruce spent the draft in what the scouts diagnosed as "QB guilt" — he passed
on nine first-round quarterbacks and then took Nix, Shough and Watson late, an
act of self-denial so severe it bordered on the ascetic. And here is the
glorious absurdity of Week 1: **Tyler Shough scored 30 points**, sixth-best in
the entire league. Add **Bijan Robinson 20**, Deshaun Watson **11**, Jeremiyah
Love **8** and Aaron Jones Sr. **8**, and the guilty conscience is 1-0.

Mitch had the pedigree at quarterback — Trevor Lawrence **26**, tenth-best
performance of the week, and Lamar Jackson **22** — plus Breece Hall **17**
and Tyler Bass **7.5**, and still came up **7.5** short. Had Tee Higgins
offered anything beyond **3**, had any single member of the herd found a
little more, this would be a different story. He is **0-1**, eleventh at
**83.5**. In the wild, the margins are always this thin, and they are always
this unfair.

---

## The Trade That Must Happen: Mike, Matt, and Chris

The fifth pairing was settled by **130.5 to 120**, Matt over Mike.

**Matt** — grade **B**, and famously the owner who drafted nineteen players and
zero tight ends — got **35** from Bryce Young, third-best in the league,
**25** from Christian Watson, **21** from Jalen Hurts, **14** from Nico
Collins, **11** from Denzel Boston. No tight end appears in his lineup,
because he does not have one. Somewhere on this same plain, **Chris** is
sitting on five of them.

**Mike** had **D'Andre Swift, 33** — fourth-best in the league — and **Justin
Jefferson, 24**, and a matched pair of fourteens from Joe Burrow and Baker
Mayfield, and **14** more from Parker Washington. It was not enough by
**10.5**. Cameron Dicker's **1** point sits in that lineup like a stone in a
shoe; Mike is **0-1**, fifth in cumulative points at **120**.

And Chris? Chris, graded **D+**, the broken-shaped creature with five tight
ends and two receivers, beat **Eli 111.5 to 91.5** by simply playing three
tight ends at once — Dallas Goedert **22**, Trey McBride **14**, Tyler Warren
**8** — alongside Jared Goff **16**, Cam Skattebo **14**, Malik Willis **14**,
Evan McPherson **9.5** and **5** from the San Francisco 49ers Defense. The
shape is wrong. The shape won.

Eli, who bet on 2028, got **24** from Jaxson Dart, **17** from Kyle Monangai,
**16** from Dak Prescott and **8** from the Pittsburgh Steelers Defense, and
learned that 2028 is still two years away. He is **0-1**, ninth at **91.5**.

---

## The State of the Plain

**1-0**: Dan, Aaron, Matt, Chris, Pete, Bruce.
**0-1**: Joe, Mike, Sean, Eli, Mitch, Cal.

There is no rank movement to report — every position on this table is a first
impression, and every matchup was a first meeting this season. Dan holds the
summit at **168** cumulative; Cal occupies the twelfth and final place at
**58**. Between them, the whole spectrum of fortune: Joe second at **137.5**
with a loss, Bruce tenth at **91** with a win. Nothing has been avenged yet,
because nothing has yet been done.
