# The French Chef Presents: Week Four in the PFL Kitchen

*Hello, I'm Julia Child.* Welcome back to the kitchen, where today we are preparing six dishes from the Peninsula Football League. Some came out of the oven puffed and golden. Some collapsed like a soufflé when the oven door got slammed. And one, my dears, landed on the floor. We shall learn from all of them. No one is born a great cook. One learns by doing, and this week a great many owners learned something.

The league average this week was **105.92 points**. Think of it as the oven temperature: anything above it was cooked through, anything below it is still a bit raw in the middle.

Put on your aprons. Here we go.

---

## Course One: The Grand Feast, Joe 148.5, Sean 136

We begin with the richest dish of the evening, because when two cooks both bring out the butter, you get a sauce worth talking about. **Joe beat Sean 148.5 to 136**, a 12.5-point margin. That was the highest-scoring kitchen of the week, and Joe's 148.5 was the best plate in the league (+28.5 against his own average).

Joe built his dish on running backs, the way you build a stew on good stock. **Brian Robinson Jr.** simmered for **27**, **Jonathan Taylor** added **22**, and **Romeo Doubs** brought **19** at receiver. Then came the quarterbacks, **Patrick Mahomes 16** and **Jordan Love 16**, with **James Cook III (14)** and **Quinshon Judkins (11)** layered in. Every element was seasoned. Joe moved **up two spots to third** in cumulative points (**480**) and is now **2-2** with a W1 streak. The "Joe's rise" storyline is coming up nicely, like dough on a warm windowsill.

Poor Sean. He cooked a beautiful **136** (+14.5 against his own average, third-best of the week) and still lost. **Chuba Hubbard** gave **25**, **Kirk Cousins** gave **24**, **Alvin Kamara 16**, **Brock Bowers 14**, **Derrick Henry 11**. Our storylines say Sean bet on running backs in a quarterback league, and this week the bet very nearly came off. He has climbed **two spots to first** in cumulative points at **486**, but his record is **1-3**. That is the most maddening thing in cooking: the best-tasting pot on the stove, and somehow it keeps getting sent back.

## Course Two: The Centerpiece, Drake Maye's 29 (Served on a Losing Plate)

Every menu needs a showpiece, and this week's is **Drake Maye**, who scored **29 points for Chris**, the top individual performance in the league. Think of a perfect roast duck carried out on a silver platter.

Then the platter went to the wrong table. **Mitch beat Chris 105 to 84.5**, a 20.5-point margin. **Jared Goff** added **20** for Chris and **Christian McCaffrey** made **11**, but after that the cupboard went thin: **Harold Fannin Jr. 8**, **Ka'imi Fairbairn 7.5**, **Michael Wilson 6**, **Cam Skattebo 3**. You remember our draft notes said Chris has five tight ends and two receivers. Well, you cannot make a bouillabaisse with five onions and two fish. That D+ draft grade is not looking unfair. Chris's **84.5** ranked **tenth** for the week, he is **1-3** on an **L3** streak (last five: WLLL), and he slipped **one spot to eighth** with **387.5**.

Mitch cooked a nice, honest supper. **Kyren Williams** scored **25**, **Lamar Jackson 16**, **Sam LaPorta 14**, **Tee Higgins 12**, **Trevor Lawrence 11**, and the **New York Giants Defense** chipped in **8**, a lovely bit of garnish you don't often get from a defense. His **105** was **+15.13** against his own average. It is Mitch's first win, so he is **1-3**, but he is still **eleventh** in points at **359.5**. One good omelette does not make a restaurant, but it is a start.

## Course Three: The Unbeaten Soufflés, Part One, Dan 113, Aaron 93.5

Now, the two C+ cooks the draft judges sniffed at are the only ones in the league whose soufflés have not fallen. **Dan beat Aaron 113 to 93.5**, a 19.5-point margin, and he is **4-0** with a **W4** streak.

The storylines say Dan "bet on 2022," and my goodness, he baked with vintage ingredients. **Kenneth Walker III** gave **28**, **Javonte Williams 27**, and **Aaron Rodgers**, aged like a good Gruyère, put up **26**. **Michael Penix Jr. (11)**, **Deebo Samuel Sr. (8)**, **Cairo Santos (7)** and **Khalil Shakir (6)** filled in the rest. Dan's 113 was actually **-3** against his own average and he dropped **two spots to fourth** in points (**464**), but the record says 4-0, and the record is what gets you a seat at the table.

Aaron's dish was undercooked. His **93.5** was **-26.63** against his own average and only **eighth** for the week. **Josh Allen (22)** and **Malik Nabers (17)** did their jobs, **Cam Ward** added **13**, but **Amon-Ra St. Brown** gave only **6** and **Chase Brown** just **3**. Aaron is now **3-1** and fell **one spot to second** with **480.5**, which is still a very respectable place on the menu.

## Course Four: The Unbeaten Soufflés, Part Two, Pete 95.5, Mike 64

**Pete beat Mike 95.5 to 64**, a 31.5-point margin, so Pete is also **4-0** with a W4 streak. His 95.5 was only **seventh** for the week (**-7.67** against his own average). It is a bit like winning the bake sale with a modest little tart because the fellow next to you dropped his cake.

**C.J. Stroud** led with **22**, **Zay Flowers** gave **17**, and **Brock Purdy** gave **16**. Then the two defenses, the **Minnesota Vikings** and **Chicago Bears**, scored **5** each, the parsley on the plate. The draft notes say Pete has the best receiver room and no backfield, and this week the backfield proved it.

Which brings us to **this week's bust**. **Najee Harris** scored **2 points for Pete**. Two! You can get more than two points out of a stale baguette. Pete won anyway, and that is the lesson: if you drop the lamb on the floor, pick it up, put it back on the platter, and remember you're alone in the kitchen. Pete sits **seventh** in points at **412.67**, down one spot, but nobody in the dining room is complaining.

As for Mike: his **64** was the **lowest score in the league** (**-26.42** against his own average). And yet **Joe Burrow** scored **28**, one of three players tied at 28 just behind Maye. Burrow made the béarnaise perfectly, and then nobody cooked the steak. **Jacoby Brissett 10**, **Tucker Kraft 8**, **Matthew Golden 8**, **Cameron Dicker 7**, **D'Andre Swift 3**. Mike is **1-3** and fell **two spots to tenth** with **361.67**.

## Course Five: The Long-Awaited Rise, Eli 128, Bruce 92.5

Bread must rise in its own time, and Eli, who the storylines say "bet on 2028," finally pulled his first loaf out of the oven. **Eli beat Bruce 128 to 92.5**, a 35.5-point margin, and his 128 was **+33** against his own average, the biggest jump of anyone this week.

**Kyle Monangai** led with **25**, **Jameis Winston** threw in **24**, **Dak Prescott** and **Puka Nacua** made **17** apiece, **George Kittle** added **11**, and **Will Reichard** kicked **10**. Eli is **1-3** with a W1 streak (last five: LLLW) and jumped **three spots to ninth** at **380**.

Bruce had two lovely ingredients, **Bijan Robinson (25)** and **Emanuel Wilson (22)**, plus **Tyler Shough** and **Deshaun Watson** at **14** each. After that the plate thinned out to **Aaron Jones Sr. 6**, then 3s from **Jeremiyah Love**, **Colston Loveland** and **KC Concepcion**, and **2.5** from kicker **Chris Boswell**. Bruce's **92.5** was **-17.5** against his own average and ninth for the week. He is **2-2** and slipped **one spot to fifth** with **440**.

## Course Six: The Blowout, Matt 137, Cal 73.5

There were **no close calls** this week, not one game decided by under 10. So for drama, I offer you the dish of the week that was carved with a cleaver. **Matt beat Cal 137 to 73.5**, a **63.5-point** margin, the largest of the week.

And here is the marvel: our storylines tell us **Matt has zero tight ends**. Imagine a coq au vin with no wine! It ought not to work. But **Tetairoa McMillan** scored **28**, **Nico Collins 25**, **Bryce Young 22**, **Ollie Gordon II 17**, **Justin Herbert 11**, and **Jake Bates** kicked **9**. Matt's 137 was second-best in the league and **+29.71** against his own average. He is **3-1** and climbed **three spots to sixth** at **429.17**. Sometimes the recipe is wrong and the dish is right anyway.

Cal, who drafted an A according to the judges, served a **73.5** (**-10.13** against his own average). **CeeDee Lamb** did his best with **20**, but **Carnell Tate 9**, **Tyler Allgeier 8**, **Ashton Jeanty 6** and **Kyler Murray 6** could not save the sauce. Cal is **1-3** and fell **two spots to last** at **334.5**. An A grade in the pantry does not mean an A on the plate.
