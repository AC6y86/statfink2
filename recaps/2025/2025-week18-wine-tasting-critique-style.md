# The 2025 Vintage, Final Pour: Week 18 Tasting Notes

*From the sommelier's notebook — the last flight of the season, decanted, swirled, and judged without mercy*

Ladies and gentlemen, we have reached the closing cork. The 2025 growing season is harvested, the barrels are dry, and what remains is one final tasting flight — six bottles, some cellar-worthy, some fit only for cooking. Records are frozen at their Week 12 vintage now; these are playoff pours, and nothing decanted this afternoon will re-label a single W or L. But the cumulative palate — the season-long accumulation of points that separates a Grand Cru from a gas-station blend — was still very much in play. Let us swirl.

## Flight One: The Nose That Nearly Fooled Us — Chris 79.5, Sean 78

A tense, tannic little number, this one, and the closest bottle on the table at a margin of 1.5. Chris, the estate that has ruled this appellation all year, uncorked a bottle that frankly underwhelmed on first sniff — 79.5 points, a full 44.89 below his own vintage average, the kind of off-note that makes a critic reach for the spit bucket. Alec Pierce provided the only genuine fruit, 25 points of bright acidity, with TreVeyon Henderson (19) adding structure and Colston Loveland (14) a touch of oak. Two DSTs, the Rams and Buccaneers, poured a flat zero apiece.

And yet. Across the table, Sean had assembled something remarkable and doomed. Rhamondre Stevenson delivered **33 points** — the single finest expression of fruit anywhere in the room this week, more on which shortly — with Parker Washington (14) and Devin Singletary (10) in support. Seventy-eight points of genuine craft, and it finished 1.5 short. Chris's Iron Grip holds: two straight wins, a league-best 2239 cumulative, and the #1 label preserved by the thinnest film of sediment. A squeaker of a finale, and the estate survives on reputation alone.

## Flight Two: A Delicate Underdog Blend — Mitch 56.67, Matt 50.5

Neither of these bottles will win a medal, but the tasting had a story. Matt had won both prior meetings this season — Week 8 and a Week 15 rout — and arrived the presumed favorite. Instead he served a thin, closed-in 50.5, a full 42.14 below his average, his week rank a dismal 11th of 12. Bryce Young (19) and C.J. Stroud (13) carried what little body there was; Darius Slayton, meanwhile, oxidized on contact for just 2 points, one of the afternoon's three flagged busts.

Mitch's 56.67 was hardly a trophy pour either — 23.26 below his own average — but Lamar Jackson's 21 points and Puka Nacua's 14 gave it just enough finish. Margin: 6.17. Mitch got the last word in a rivalry he'd been losing all season, and the little underdog blend takes the flight.

## Flight Three: The Corked Bottle — Dan 80, Mike 43

Every tasting has one bottle you pour straight down the sink, and here it is. Mike's 43 points were faulted, flabby, and 40.83 below his average — dead last in the week's rankings at 12th of 12, the very definition of feast-or-famine finishing in famine. Jahmyr Gibbs (14) and Baker Mayfield (11) were the only drinkable notes; Kyle Monangai corked at 2, and both DSTs poured nothing.

Dan, by contrast, decanted a clean, generous 80 — the week's widest margin at 37. Zay Flowers led with 25 points of vibrant fruit, and in a delightful surprise the Browns DST returned 16, a rich mineral streak you rarely find in defensive bottling. Aaron Rodgers added 14. Not a Grand Cru, but a confident, well-made table wine that embarrassed its neighbor.

## Flight Four: Two Thin Whites, One Slightly Less Thin — Eli 55.17, Joe 53

Pour these side by side and you'd struggle to tell them apart — both pale, both restrained, both a little watery. Joe, our season-long runner-up, served the more disappointing bottle: 53 points, an astonishing 61.75 below his own vintage average, the harshest self-comparison anywhere on the table. Jacoby Brissett (16) and Cade Otton (14) were serviceable; the rest evaporated. This is Joe's Late Fade in a glass — a 10-2 label that can no longer disguise what's actually in the bottle.

Eli edged it, 55.17, on the strength of Tyler Shough's 22-point vintage and Kenneth Gainwell (11). Margin: 2.17 — the second-closest pour of the day. A modest bottle wins a modest flight, and Joe's distant-second cumulative of 2065.5 sits, unmoved, in the shadow of Chris's 2239.

## Flight Five: The Grand Cru of the Afternoon — Cal 108.5, Pete 85.67

Now *this* is what we came to taste. Cal's 108.5 was the finest bottle in the entire cellar this week, a full-bodied, layered, textbook expression — and, at +0.14 versus his own average, exactly the wine the estate promises. Trevor Lawrence poured 24, Tyrone Tracy Jr. 20, Caleb Williams 18 — three players over 10 points forming a trifecta of ripe, structured fruit — with Drake London (14) and Chris Rodriguez Jr. (11) lengthening the finish. Even Keon Coleman's faulted 2-point bust couldn't sour it. This is Cal's Closing Kick, and it vaults the estate into third at 1950.5 cumulative.

Pete's 85.67 was no swill — Matthew Stafford's 29 was the week's second-best individual note, Joe Burrow added 21, Ray Davis 20 — but against Cal's bottle it was outclassed by 22.83. A fine wine served at the wrong table. Pete does climb one rung to fifth in the cellar rankings; small consolation for a three-loss finishing skid.

## Flight Six: A Robust, Confident Red — Bruce 93, Aaron 66.5

We close on a well-structured pour. Bruce's 93 completed a season sweep of Aaron — Weeks 7, 15, and now 18, a third straight double-digit win — and capped Bruce's Late Surge at three victories running. Riley Leonard was the star of the barrel with **27 points**, the week's third-best individual, joined by Ja'Marr Chase (14), Michael Wilson (14), and a tidy 8 from the Texans DST. Aaron's 66.5 had honest fruit — Jaxson Dart (16), Tank Bigsby (14), Ka'imi Fairbairn's 13 — but 26.5 points short is 26.5 points short. Aaron's lost season closes at the bottom of the cellar, 1331.5, on an L2 skid.

## The Sommelier's Final Verdict

Three flights decided by single-digit margins — 1.5, 2.17, 6.17 — and yet the vintage of 2025 finishes exactly as the label promised: Chris on top at 2239, Joe second at 2065.5, Cal's late-harvest 108.5 securing third at 1950.5. The week's average bottle scored 70.79. The wine of the day, though, was pressed by a losing estate: Rhamondre Stevenson's 33 points for Sean, a Grand Cru fruit born in a bottle that finished 1.5 short. Sometimes the finest note plays in the losing pour. That, friends, is the whole cruel poetry of the tasting.

The barrels are empty. The 2025 vintage is corked and cellared. We reconvene next season.
