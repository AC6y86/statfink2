# Commencement Address to the Peninsula Football League, Class of 2025

*Delivered at the close of Week 18. Please hold your applause — and your waiver claims — until the end.*

---

Distinguished owners, honored guests, and one gentleman in the back who is only here for the free buffet:

Welcome. Take your seats. Adjust your caps. Because today we gather not merely to end a season, but to *commence* one's ending — for "commencement," you'll recall from a class you probably slept through, does not mean a finish. It means a beginning. And there is no beginning quite so bittersweet as the one that arrives after eighteen long weeks, when the tassel finally swings from one side of the mortarboard to the other and we send you, our graduates, out into the cruel and coverage-optional world of the offseason.

Look to your left. Look to your right. This week, a league-wide average of just **70.79 points** says that at least one of the people you're looking at scored like they'd already stopped studying. Let us hand out the diplomas.

## Valedictorian, and the Quiet Kind of Greatness

Every class has its valedictorian, and ours graduated summa cum laude by the narrowest of margins. **Chris (79.5) defeated Sean (78) by 1.5** — not a landslide, not a coronation, but a squeaker of a final exam that sealed the league-high **2239 cumulative points** and the No. 1 seat on this stage. It was, fittingly, unglamorous work. **Alec Pierce delivered 25**, **TreVeyon Henderson added 19**, **Colston Loveland chipped in 14** — and Chris posted a number **44.89 below his own season average** and *still* walked away the finale's winner. That, graduates, is what tenure looks like. Chris rode a two-game win streak (last five: WWLWW) and closed the Sean series he opened back in Week 5 (98-91). Iron grips do not need to be pretty. They only need to hold.

And oh, how Sean nearly crashed the ceremony. **Rhamondre Stevenson poured in 33 points** — the single highest individual score of the entire week, our surprise commencement speaker who showed up, brought down the house, and then watched the diploma go to someone else. **Parker Washington (14)** and **Devin Singletary (10)** did their part. But 78 in a losing effort is the oldest lesson in the yearbook: sometimes you do everything right and still don't make valedictorian by a point and a half.

## The Class Overachiever

If Chris is your valedictorian, **Cal** is the student who peaked gloriously on the very last day. His **108.5 was the highest score in the building** — a beatdown of **Pete, 108.5 to 85.67, by 22.83** — and it vaulted him from fourth into **third at 1950.5 cumulative points**. This was a group project done right: **Trevor Lawrence 24, Tyrone Tracy Jr. 20, Caleb Williams 18, Drake London 14, Chris Rodriguez Jr. 11.** Five contributors, a score **0.14 above his own average**, and a closing kick worthy of a standing ovation. Pete graduated with honor, too — **Matthew Stafford's 29** was the second-best individual mark of the week, **Joe Burrow added 21, Ray Davis another 20** — but 85.67 wasn't enough, and Pete leaves us on an L3 skid (last five WWLLL), moving up one rank to fifth on cumulative points at 1692.67 all the same.

## The Late Bloomer

Some students find themselves only in their final semester. **Bruce** is our late bloomer, and he graduated on a three-game heater (WLWWW), dispatching **Aaron 93 to 66.5, by 26.5**. **Riley Leonard's 27** anchored the ceremony — the third-highest individual score of the week — with **Ja'Marr Chase and Michael Wilson matching at 14 apiece** and even the **Texans DST posting 8**. It completes a season sweep of Aaron: Week 7, Week 15, and now the finale, a third straight double-digit win. For Aaron, our most patient graduate at **2-10**, the season closes as it lived — an L2 skid, a **66.5** that even **Jaxson Dart's 16** and **Ka'imi Fairbairn's 13** couldn't rescue. Aaron, the diploma still counts. You showed up every week. That is more than the syllabus asked.

## The Biggest Margin, and the Feast-or-Famine Grad

The widest final grade gap of the day belonged to **Dan, who trounced Mike 80 to 43** — a **37-point** margin, the blowout of the week. **Zay Flowers went for 25**, the **Browns DST added a remarkable 16**, and **Aaron Rodgers contributed 14** as Dan ended his season on the up (last five LLLLW). Mike, meanwhile, gave us the feast-or-famine finale we've come to expect: **43 points, dead last in the week's rankings, 12 of 12**, a full **40.83 below his own average.** **Jahmyr Gibbs (14)** tried; **Kyle Monangai's 2 points** landed him on our bust list alongside **Cal's Keon Coleman (2)** and **Matt's Darius Slayton (2)**. Even valedictorian-caliber classes have a few who cram and still blank the final.

## The Heartbreak Ceremony

Now, graduates, sit still, because two of you left this room in tears. **Eli edged Joe, 55.17 to 53, by 2.17** — and what a cruel diploma to hand out. **Tyler Shough's 22** and **Kenneth Gainwell's 11** got Eli across the stage. Joe? Our second-place finisher, our 10-2 stalwart, scored **53 — a full 61.75 below his own average**, the deepest plunge of the entire week. **Jacoby Brissett (16)** and **Cade Otton (14)** couldn't paper over it. Joe's late fade is now a matter of record: second at **2065.5**, a real and growing gap behind Chris, and a finale that read more like a senior who'd already mailed it in. The what-if writes itself — one more field goal, one more catch, and Joe walks instead of Eli. But the transcript is the transcript.

And in the day's other cliffhanger, **Mitch got the last word on Matt, 56.67 to 50.5, margin 6.17.** Matt had swept their series (Week 8, Week 15), but **Lamar Jackson's 21**, **Puka Nacua's 14**, and even the **Vikings DST's 6.67** flipped the script. Matt's **50.5** — **42.14 below his average** — dropped him one rank to sixth (1667.5). A cautionary tale, dear class: past performance guarantees nothing on graduation day.

## Conferral of Final Standings

By the authority vested in eighteen weeks of frozen playoff records, I hereby confer the final honors: **Chris (10-2) atop the class at 2239. Joe (10-2) second at 2065.5. Cal (7-5) third at 1950.5**, our closing-kick honoree who leapfrogged into the money. Behind them, **Bruce (4th, 1753), Pete (5th, ▲1), Matt (6th, ▼1)**, and down through **Sean, Dan, Mike, Mitch, Eli, and Aaron** anchoring at 1331.5. Remember, graduates — these games did not move your records; in the playoff weeks, those were sealed at Week 12. What moved was your legacy.
