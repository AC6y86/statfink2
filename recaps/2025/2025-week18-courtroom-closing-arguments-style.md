# The People v. Week 18: Closing Arguments

*Delivered before the jury of the Peninsula Football League. The Honorable Season 2025 presiding. Let the record reflect: this is a PLAYOFF week. Win-loss records are FROZEN at their Week 12 values, and no attorney in this courtroom will argue otherwise. The verdicts that matter today are written in points.*

---

Ladies and gentlemen of the jury.

For eighteen weeks you have sat in that box. You have heard the testimony, weighed the exhibits, and watched grown men live and die by the box score. Today I ask you to do one final thing: examine the evidence of Week 18 — the finale, the last word, the closing statement of an entire season — and render your verdict on each of six counts.

I remind you of the standard. Not "probable." Not "he should have won." The standard in this court is what actually happened, entered into the record, beyond a reasonable doubt. Let us begin.

## Count One: The People v. Chris — a conviction by a hair

The State's marquee case, and I will not oversell it, because the margin does not permit exaggeration. Sean scored 78. Chris scored 79.5. One and one-half points. That, members of the jury, is the width of a fingerprint.

The defense will tell you Sean was robbed, and I'll grant them their star witness: Rhamondre Stevenson took the stand with **33 points** — the single largest individual performance anywhere in this league in Week 18, the top score on the docket. But one witness does not win a trial. Parker Washington corroborated with 14 and Devin Singletary added 10, and still it was not enough, because Chris's Alec Pierce answered with **25**, TreVeyon Henderson delivered **19**, and Colston Loveland chipped in **14**.

Here is the damning part. Chris scored 79.5 — a full **44.89 below his own season average**. He convicted on his worst evidence of the year, and it still held up. That is not luck, ladies and gentlemen. That is a champion. His **Iron Grip** tightens: two straight wins, and a league-high **2,239 cumulative points** — the No. 1 seal on the entire season, entered into the record and admissible for all time. The prosecution rests.

## Count Two: The People v. Mitch — the last word in a bitter feud

Exhibit: the history. Matt beat Mitch in Week 8, beat him again in Week 15 by thirty-one. A pattern of dominance, counsel would argue. But the finale is where alibis go to die. Mitch 56.67, Matt 50.5 — a margin of 6.17, and the rebuttal witness was Lamar Jackson with **21**, Puka Nacua with **14**, and Jake Bates kicking **8.5**.

The defense's case collapsed under its own weight. Matt mustered 50.5 — **42.14 below his own average** — with Darius Slayton caught red-handed at **2 points**, one of the three certified busts of the week. Bryce Young's 19 and C.J. Stroud's 13 were not enough to overcome the testimony. Mitch got the last word in the season series. Objection overruled.

## Count Three: The People v. Dan — the blowout of the week

When there is no close call to agonize over, ladies and gentlemen, we do not manufacture drama — we savor the demolition. Dan 80, Mike 43. A margin of **37**, the largest on any docket this week.

The evidence was overwhelming. Zay Flowers testified to **25 points**, the Browns DST added a staggering **16**, and Aaron Rodgers submitted **14**. Against that mountain, the defense offered Jahmyr Gibbs at 14 and Baker Mayfield at 11 — and then went silent. Mike posted 43, dead last in the week (rank 12 of 12), **40.83 below his own average**, with Kyle Monangai logged at **2 points** for the bust record. His **Feast-or-Famine** pattern needs no closing argument; the jury has seen it all year. Guilty on all counts.

## Count Four: The People v. Eli — the two-point heist

Now we come to the cruelest exhibit in the file. Joe — the man who spent this season shoulder to shoulder with the champion — walked into the finale and scored **53 points**, a stunning **61.75 below his own average**, the largest negative delta anywhere in this courtroom.

Eli seized the opening. 55.17 to 53. A margin of 2.17. Tyler Shough delivered **22**, Kenneth Gainwell **11**, Wil Lutz kicked **8.5**, and the theft was complete. Joe's Jacoby Brissett managed 16 and Cade Otton 14, but the cupboard was bare behind them. This, members of the jury, is **Joe's Late Fade** entered as physical evidence — a 10-2 record that means nothing when the cumulative gap to Chris (2,239 to 2,065.5) is real and growing. Joe's second-place finish is secured, but he closes on a loss, and the record will show it.

## Count Five: The People v. Cal — the closing kick

If Count One was won on the worst evidence, Count Five was won on the best. Cal 108.5, Pete 85.67 — a margin of 22.83, and the highest score in the entire league this week, delivered **right on his own average** (+0.14). A man peaking at the buzzer.

Consider the breadth of the testimony: Trevor Lawrence **24**, Tyrone Tracy Jr. **20**, Caleb Williams **18**, Drake London **14**, Chris Rodriguez Jr. **11** — five witnesses in double digits or better. Yes, Keon Coleman was flagged as a bust at **2 points**, but when your other witnesses are that credible, one weak alibi is immaterial. **Cal's Closing Kick** is no longer a theory — it vaults him into third at **1,950.5 cumulative**. Pete, for his part, was not disgraced: Matthew Stafford's **29** was the second-best individual score of the week and Joe Burrow added **21**, Ray Davis **20**. Pete even climbs one rank to fifth. But the higher total wins, and Cal had it in abundance.

## Count Six: The People v. Bruce — the season sweep

The final count, and the file is thick. Bruce has now beaten Aaron in Week 7, Week 15, and Week 18 — a clean sweep, three straight double-digit convictions. Bruce 93, Aaron 66.5, margin 26.5.

The lead witness was Riley Leonard with **27 points** — third-best individual score of the week — flanked by Ja'Marr Chase and Michael Wilson, each with **14**, and the Texans DST with **8**. **Bruce's Late Surge** is now three straight wins (WLWWW) and a fourth-place cumulative finish. Aaron, meanwhile, closes **Aaron's Lost Season** exactly as the season foretold: 2-10, an L2 skid, anchored at the bottom of the standings at 1,331.5, despite honest work from Jaxson Dart (16) and Ka'imi Fairbairn (13). Sometimes, ladies and gentlemen, the evidence simply is what it is.

## The Verdict on the Season

The jury's final findings, now sealed for 2025: Chris, No. 1, **2,239 points**. Joe, No. 2, **2,065.5**. Cal, No. 3, **1,950.5**. The gavel has fallen. Court is adjourned for the offseason.
